# P580: real-time effects on the S3's PIE (spike finding, 2026-11-02)

Spark issue [#771](https://github.com/xmejkal/spark/issues/771), direction D2 of the games restyle (docs/discovery/2026-11-02-games-restyle/brief.md:124). Reads p537-finding.md, bench.md P556 and P567 (run46), s3lcd/src/frame.c, s3lcd.c. Refuted and fixed 2026-11-02 (p580-refuter.md, 4 blocking and 8 advisory findings). Tags: E evidence with a pointer, H a hypothesis and where it comes from, D a decision proposed here. All web sources read 2026-11-02. Clock: 240 MHz (E: build-s3lcd/sdkconfig:1592 `CONFIG_ESP_DEFAULT_CPU_FREQ_MHZ=240`, games/ sets none. one cycle = 4.17 ns). The C baseline builds with `CONFIG_COMPILER_OPTIMIZATION_PERF`, -O2 (E: the same sdkconfig).

## Summary

> 1. PIE is a 128-bit SIMD unit with 8 Q registers of 16 x 8-bit or 8 x 16-bit lanes. It has saturating add and sub, and multiply with a shift. It compares in S8, S16 and S32 lanes, and it has no gather. Loads and stores are 16-byte aligned, with a three-instruction pattern for unaligned data. GCC does not know the Q registers. So the kernels are `.S` files called from C, as Espressif does in esp-dsp and esp_lvgl_port.
> 2. The compose, not the kernel, is the cost. An alpha blend costs 337.5 ns/px into the PSRAM frame and 323.4 ns/px into internal RAM (run46), so compute dominates, and PIE pays on the blend: about 100 ns/px (H). A PSRAM write adds 55 ns/px and a read 34 (run46 "double" against "dma"). Full-screen effects cost 8 to 26 ms a frame and fit only stack's game over.
> 3. Best three, with the compose counted. A heat-diffusion kernel, fire and smoke from one kernel: a 320 x 40 strip about 1.8 ms alpha or 1.2 ms keyed on PIE, 5.9 ms in C. Particles in C: sparks, dust, debris, 64 of them about 0.2 ms blended (40 cost 2.27 ms in Python today). PIE adds nothing at this count. A windowed ripple or shockwave: 64 x 64, about 0.8 ms. Its gather stays scalar, so PIE gains only on the wave update.
> 4. The API: one call starts an effect at a game x, y with a few numbers. The C layer steps and draws it each frame, and the game never reads anything back. That bends TL-15 from "draws, decides nothing" to "draws, decides nothing the game reads", and the PO's yes on it (Q13) gates slice 1. Every kernel has a plain C twin, and the `.S` file is a build flag.
> 5. Plan: slice 0 asks whether a `.S` assembles and what the compose costs today, then times the heat kernel and the compose, C against PIE, with a cold cache. Every outcome has a verdict. Then particles in C, then the kernel in stack RENT, then the ripple. Each has a kill line.

## Question 1: what PIE offers for pixel work

Sources. The TRM v1.8, chapter 1 "Processor Instruction Extensions (PIE)" (the espressif.com PDF, sha256 4484bf8a69035ec42a731c58c64ada6fbd1f1618c5559409f134d9ea083f444f, 1,531 pages, read by the refuter. my pages are the documentation.espressif.com anchors, read 2026-11-02): 1.1 Overview and 1.3 Structure p. 39, 1.3.5 Address Unit p. 41, 1.5.1 Registers p. 45, SAR_BYTE p. 47, 1.5.3 Data Format and Alignment p. 48, 1.6.5 Comparison Instructions p. 60, 1.8.79 EE.VCMP.EQ.S16 p. 155, 1.8.82 EE.VCMP.GT.S16 p. 158, 1.8.85 EE.VCMP.LT.S16 p. 161, 1.8.88 EE.VLD.128.IP p. 164. Espressif's cheat sheet in esp-dl, [esp32s3-pie-simd/SKILL.md](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl/blob/master/tools/agents/skills/esp32s3-pie-simd/SKILL.md) and its `references/pie_instruction_set.md` (version 1.0, 2026-04-02). All E unless marked.

- Registers: 8 QR registers q0 to q7, 128 bits, read as 16 x 8-bit, 8 x 16-bit or 4 x 32-bit lanes. SAR (6-bit) holds the shift after a multiply, SAR_BYTE (4-bit) the byte shift for unaligned data. ACCX is a 40-bit scalar accumulator, QACC_H and QACC_L 160-bit per-lane accumulators. Sixteen 8-bit multipliers, eight 16-bit (TRM p. 39, 45).
- Arithmetic: EE.VADDS and EE.VSUBS saturating in S8, S16, S32, with fused load (.LD.INCP) and store (.ST.INCP) forms. EE.VMUL in S8, S16, U8, U16, result shifted right by SAR, no saturation, no 32-bit multiply. EE.VMULAS multiply-accumulate into ACCX or QACC, extracted with saturation by EE.SRCMB. EE.VMAX, EE.VMIN in S8, S16, S32. EE.VCMP.EQ, LT, GT in S8, S16 and S32, giving all-ones lane masks for EE.ANDQ, ORQ, XORQ, NOTQ (TRM 1.6.5 p. 60, 1.8.79, 1.8.82, 1.8.85). The esp-dl sheet lists S8 only, a gap in the sheet, which the first version of this finding copied. So a keyed RGB565 compose fits PIE: VCMP.EQ.S16 against the key, then ANDQ and ORQ select. Shifts: EE.VSL.32 and EE.VSR.32 on 32-bit lanes only, EE.SRC.Q the byte funnel shift. a 16-bit lane shift is done as EE.VMUL.U16 by a constant with SAR (D: the trick the blend and repack kernels use). EE.VZIP and VUNZIP interleave lanes (the cheat sheet's quick reference). No gather or scatter: a palette or a displacement lookup stays scalar (D, from the absence in the instruction list).
- Loads and stores: EE.VLD.128.IP and .XP, EE.VST.128.IP and .XP, 64-bit halves, broadcasts EE.VLDBC.8, 16, 32. Every access forces its low address bits to zero, 4 bits for 128-bit. So an unaligned pointer silently reads the aligned block before it (TRM p. 48). Unaligned data: EE.LD.128.USAR.IP saves the offset in SAR_BYTE, EE.VLD.128.IP loads the next block, EE.SRC.Q joins them (TRM p. 47, 48). Buffers get `__attribute__((aligned(16)))` or `heap_caps_aligned_alloc(16, ...)` (TRM p. 48). Found: the cheat sheet gives EE.VLD.128.IP's step as -512..496 and the TRM p. 164 as -2048..2032. Trust the TRM.
- Hazards: a QR load to its use costs 2 cycles, a SAR write needs one instruction gap or ISYNC (cheat sheet, "PIPELINE & SCHEDULING").
- PSRAM: no source says PIE loads from PSRAM behave differently from scalar loads. both go through the data cache (H). A trap report on esp32.com was a 128-bit load over the instruction bus, answered by Espressif: the data must be in data-accessible memory ([search result](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=104330), the thread itself was not readable: bot check). The frame is 150 KB, past the 32 KB cache, so a kernel that reads it runs at PSRAM speed whatever the instruction (E: p537-finding.md, "PSRAM". our 89 ns/px copy). Slice 0 measures a PIE load from PSRAM against internal, one number.
- Calling it from a MicroPython user C module. GCC emits no PIE instruction and does not know q0 to q7 or ACCX, so they cannot be inline-asm operands or clobbers (E: Espressif staff on esp32.com via the [search](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=146698), not read in full). Espressif's blog shows both a `.S` routine and `asm volatile` blocks whose Q registers the compiler never sees ([PIE introduction](https://developer.espressif.com/blog/2024/12/pie-introduction/), Yan Ke, 2024-12-05). its numbers are on an ESP32-P4, not ours. Every Espressif production use is a `.S` file with the windowed ABI (`entry`, args in a2 to a7, `retw`): esp-dsp's `dspi_dotprod_s16_aes3.S` (ee.vld.128.ip, ee.ld.128.usar.ip, ee.vmulas.s16.accx.ld.ip.qup, rur.accx_0, behind `#if dspi_dotprod_aes3_enabled`, with a fallback to the ansi routine for a length that is not a multiple of 8), esp_lvgl_port's `lv_color_blend_to_rgb565_esp32s3.S` (ee.movi.32.q, ee.vst.128.ip, ee.src.q with wur.sar_byte, four alignment paths, `loopnez`) and `lv_rgb565_blend_normal_to_rgb565_esp32s3.S` (an opaque copy, 16 or 24 px per iteration, no alpha). Larry Bank's minimal example is a `.S` too ([bitbank, 2024-01-31](https://bitbanksoftware.blogspot.com/2024/01/esp32-s3-simd-minimal-example.html)). Clang has `__builtin_xtensa_ee_*` intrinsics (search result, esp-rs issue 190). MicroPython builds with GCC, so not for us. Our module lists its sources in `target_sources(usermod_s3lcd INTERFACE ...)` (s3lcd/src/micropython.cmake). ESP-IDF projects enable ASM, so a `.S` added there is expected to assemble (H: esp_lvgl_port and esp-dsp build this way as IDF components. A user C module is untested, and slice 0 proves it). Flags: none special. the assembler of xtensa-esp32s3-elf accepts the `ee.*` mnemonics (E: the `.S` files above build in IDF).

## Question 2: the costs behind the table

- The one measured graphics number on PIE: esp_lvgl_port's RGB565 fill 0.196 cycles/px against 1.146 in C (5.8 x), copy 0.352 against 3.437 (9.8 x), at 128 x 128 aligned. unaligned fill gains 2.3 x (E: [simd README](https://github.com/espressif/esp-bsp/blob/master/components/esp_lvgl_port/test_apps/simd/README.md), clock and memory not stated. the ratios are mine). No published number exists for an alpha blend, a colour key, a blur or a cellular kernel on PIE. those rows are H from instruction counts.
- Our compose floor (E: run46 both logs, bench.md P567. s3lcd.c:186): the full-frame copy costs 2.58 ms into the internal frame and 6.83 ms into the PSRAM frame, so a PSRAM write costs about 55 ns/px and a read about 34. The 64 x 64 alpha blend costs 337.5 ns/px into PSRAM and 323.4 into internal RAM, 81 and 78 cycles. So the blend is compute, not memory: `alpha_blend_565` unpacks, multiplies and repacks per pixel. A PIE blend on 8 lanes is about 25 instructions per 8 px, 3 cycles/px, plus the PSRAM traffic: about 100 ns/px (H). The lesson: PIE pays on the compose, and the kernel is small either way.
- The frame budget: pace 40 ms, paint 39 to 43 ms in the scrolling games and about 16 ms in stack (E: bench.md P556). The scrolling games have no room until P554 (kept background) and P537 slices 2 and 3 land. stack has about 20 ms. So the first game is stack RENT, and every effect is a window or a strip, not a screen.
- Fire: Lode's algorithm, `fire[y][x] = (sum of below-left, below, below-right, two-below) * 32 / 129`, a random bottom row each frame, a 256-entry palette ramping red to yellow in hue and up in lightness (E: [lodev.org/cgtutor/fire.html](https://lodev.org/cgtutor/fire.html)). On PIE: three row loads, two of them through the unaligned pattern or a padded row. EE.VADDS.S8 saturates at 127, so use U16 lanes, or 8-bit lanes with a halving first. The x32/129 becomes EE.VMUL.U16 by 63 with SAR 8 (x63/256, a cooler flame, D). Smoke is the same kernel with a grey-blue palette, a slower decay and a sideways drift (D: one kernel, two palettes). The art rule ART-3 says soft smoke cannot be a sprite. it is baked onto a known background or drawn as particles and fills (E: council/art-direction.md:18-20). This kernel is a third way, soft and moving. Panel order (E: lcd.py:59, game x runs along panel y): a 320 x 40 game strip is 320 panel rows of 40 px, 80 B, five 16 B vectors per row. Heat flows along game y, within a panel row. The heat scratch is kept in panel order, and its game y offset rounds to 8 px, so every row is 16-byte aligned (D).
- Ripples: Hugo Elias's two-buffer scheme as the Microsoft archive relays it: `Buffer2[x,y] = (Buffer1[x-1,y] + Buffer1[x+1,y] + Buffer1[x,y+1] + Buffer1[x,y-1]) / 2 - Buffer2[x,y]`, then damping, swap. render with `Xoffset = buffer[x-1,y] - buffer[x+1,y]`, `Yoffset = buffer[x,y-1] - buffer[x,y+1]`, `t = texture[x+Xoffset, y+Yoffset]` plus a shading term (E: [learn.microsoft.com archive, 2008](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/blogs/nikola/building-the-reallistic-water-ripples-sample-in-silverlight), citing Elias's page, which is offline). The damping as `x - (x >> 5)` keeps it integer (D). The texture is the background under the window. So the effect needs a snapshot at start (8 KB for 64 x 64), or the kept background of P554.
- Glow: three box blurs approximate a Gaussian within 0.04 % per pixel, and a running-sum box blur is O(n) per row regardless of radius (E: [ivank.net](https://blog.ivank.net/fastest-gaussian-blur.html)). A running sum is serial, so PIE helps a fixed 3 x 3 kernel, not a wide radius (D).
- Noise: Perlin's improved noise uses a 256-entry permutation, 12 gradients and the fade `6t^5 - 15t^4 + 10t^3` (E: [mrl.cs.nyu.edu/~perlin/noise](https://mrl.cs.nyu.edu/~perlin/noise/)). Value noise with bilinear lerp on int16 lanes is the cheaper fit here. it lost to the heat kernel on code size for the same look (D).

## Question 4: the API shape and TL-15

The game starts an effect with one call in game coordinates and forgets it:

```python
h = tft.fx(tft.FX_SMOKE, x, y, w=64, h=64, strength=200, life=30)   # a handle, or None when the slot table is full
tft.fx(tft.FX_BURST, x, y, n=24, speed=90, colour=RUST)              # particles
tft.fx(tft.FX_RIPPLE, x, y, w=64, h=64, radius=6)
tft.fx_stop(h)                                                        # early end. restore runs
tft.show()                                                            # steps every live effect by the frame time, draws it, then sends
```

- Where it sits: `fx.c` beside `frame.c`, with `frame_t` as its dependency (pixels, width, height, `frame_dirty`), no esp_lcd call, as p537's `scene.c` plan. The orientation map (lcd.py `TftCanvas`, game x, y to panel y, x) moves into the call, as P537 slice 3 plans anyway. until then `fx()` takes panel coordinates and `TftCanvas.fx()` maps them, 5 lines of Python.
- Slots: a fixed table of about 4 effects, each with its kind, window and time left. Each claims its scratch from one 16-byte aligned internal arena allocated at `init()`. A 64 x 64 ripple alone takes 24,576 B, so the arena proposed is about 18.4 KB: a 48 x 48 ripple (13,824 B), one 64 x 64 heat buffer (4,096 B) and a palette (512 B) (D). The same 31,744 B block also carries TL-10's claims for P554, P537 and P557, so the split is the tech lead's call. No allocation per frame.
- TL-15 says "the layer draws and decides nothing" and names a particle engine in C as a direction change (E: council/tech-lead.md:60). D2 directs the effects in C. The bend proposed: an effect is a drawing that evolves in time. The C layer owns its state and clock, and the game never reads anything back: no collision, no score, no event comes from an effect. The game's speeds and rules stay in Python. So the rule becomes "draws, decides nothing the game reads" (D). TL-15 names "particles' motion" as physics, so this is a change of the rule, not a reading of it, and the PO's yes on Q13 gates slice 1. Gravity, drag and speeds are passed in from Python on every call, never constants in C (D). The game's own `Particles` class (gfx.py:233) stays for anything the rules read. `FX_BURST` is for looks.
- Fallback: every kernel exists in plain C first (`fx_fire_c`, `fx_ripple_c`), and a `.S` twin is chosen by `#if defined(CONFIG_IDF_TARGET_ESP32S3) && FX_PIE`. The Mac tests run against the fake driver, which draws nothing for `fx()`. A board without PIE gets the C twin at the costs in the table's C column. D2 says "S3 only". the C twin costs nothing extra because slice 0 writes it as the baseline.
- Platform-neutral edge: `fx(kind, x, y, **numbers)` names no chip, no instruction and no buffer. the P539 library can carry the same signature on another core with only the C twin.

## Question 5: the sliced plan

Each slice is one PR in firebeetle-games, measured on the board, with its number in bench.md and a kill line. Clock printed (`machine.freq()`) in every run.

0. **Board probe: does PIE pay on one effect?** Two cheap tests first, no game change: a 10-line `.S` with one `ee.*` instruction listed in micropython.cmake (does it assemble and run), and `blit_buffer` of a 40 x 320 panel window at alpha 128 through the existing path, cold (what the compose costs today). Then `s3lcd.fx_bench(op, w, h, reps)`: the 64 x 64 heat kernel and the alpha and keyed compose, each in plain C and in a `.S`, 200 reps, cycles per pixel from `esp_cpu_get_cycle_count()`. Cold cache: a full-frame blit between reps, as probe567 did (the data cache is 32 KB, `CONFIG_ESP32S3_DATA_CACHE_SIZE=0x8000`, so a warm 64 x 64 window hides the PSRAM cost: run46 shows 23.76 µs warm against 67.80 interleaved for one sprite). Also the kernel reading its buffer in PSRAM, and the palette map alone. Then the 320 x 40 strip end to end, panel order, both compose modes. Verdicts, proposed: PIE under 1.5 x on the compose: drop the `.S`, all C, and D2 is answered "PIE does not pay here". 1.5 x to 3 x: the `.S` stays for the compose only, the heat kernel stays C. 3 x or more: the `.S` stays for both, and slice 2 builds on PIE. Strip at or under the line (1.5 ms keyed, 2.5 ms alpha): slice 2 uses the strip. Over it: slice 2 uses a 64 x 64 window and re-measures. Every number goes to bench.md. About 200 lines of C and asm.
1. **Particles in C.** Gate: the PO's yes on the TL-15 bend (Q13), recorded as Q13's answer. `FX_BURST` and `FX_DUST`: SoA int16, 64 slots, gravity and drag from the call, 2 x 2 or 3 x 3 dots, additive or opaque, plain C (PIE waits for a count past 1,000). `Particles.draw` keeps working for the games that read particles. Measures: update plus draw of 64 particles against today's 2.27 ms for 40. Pass: under 0.3 ms, no stale dot after the last particle dies (dirty tiles marked). Testable on the board with `probe`-style timing and by eye.
2. **Fire and smoke in stack RENT.** The heat kernel from slice 0 behind `FX_SMOKE` and `FX_FIRE` with two palettes: a dust puff at each drop (64 x 32 at the landing edge), and the tower-fall dust at game over (a 320 x 40 strip). That strip is P568's "dust" (E: council/art-direction.md:163). Measures: ms per frame with and without the effect, fps in the `time` line. Pass: D3's line, fps at most 2 below stack's own 23 to 24, the strip under slice 0's line, and the PO's words. Kill line: over 3 ms or his "no", the strip shrinks to a window or goes.
3. **Ripple or shockwave window.** `FX_RIPPLE` 64 x 64 with a background snapshot and a restore at the end. First on the perfect drop's gold flash in stack (as the shockwave), later on HIGH WATER's surface. Measures: ms per frame, stale pixels after the restore through three drops.
