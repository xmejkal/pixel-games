# P580: real-time effects on the S3's PIE (spike finding, 2026-11-02)

Spark issue [#771](https://github.com/xmejkal/spark/issues/771), direction D2 of the games restyle (docs/discovery/2026-11-02-games-restyle/brief.md:124). Reads p537-finding.md, bench.md P556 and P567 (run46), s3lcd/src/frame.c, s3lcd.c. Tags: E evidence with a pointer, H a hypothesis and where it comes from, D a decision proposed here. All web sources read 2026-11-02. Clock: 240 MHz assumed (H: the S3 default. `machine.freq()` is not in any log. one cycle = 4.17 ns).

## Summary

> 1. PIE is a 128-bit SIMD unit with 8 Q registers of 16 x 8-bit or 8 x 16-bit lanes. It has saturating add and sub, and multiply with a shift. It has no gather and no 16-bit compare. Loads and stores are 16-byte aligned, with a three-instruction pattern for unaligned data. GCC does not know the Q registers. So the kernels are `.S` files called from C, as Espressif does in esp-dsp and esp_lvgl_port.
> 2. The frame lives in PSRAM, and that caps every gain. Our full-frame copy costs 89 ns/px (6.83 ms / 76,800 px, run46), about 21 cycles. PIE computes in 0.2 to 4 cycles/px in internal RAM. So an effect computes in a small internal window and touches the PSRAM frame once. Full-screen effects cost 8 to 15 ms a frame and fit only stack.
> 3. Best three. A heat-diffusion kernel: fire and smoke from one kernel, 8-bit lanes, about 1.2 ms for a 320 x 40 strip. Particles in C: sparks, dust, debris. Today 40 cost 2.3 ms in Python, 64 in C cost about 0.1 ms, and PIE adds nothing at this count. A windowed ripple or shockwave: 64 x 64, about 0.8 ms. Its gather stays scalar, so PIE gains only on the wave update.
> 4. The API: one call starts an effect at a game x, y with a few numbers. The C layer steps and draws it each frame, and the game never reads anything back. That bends TL-15 from "draws, decides nothing" to "draws, decides nothing the game reads". Every kernel has a plain C twin, and the `.S` file is a build flag.
> 5. Plan: slice 0 is a 64 x 64 heat kernel in C against PIE on the board. Its pass line: PIE at least 3 x on the internal kernel, the whole strip under 1.5 ms. Then particles in C, then the kernel in stack RENT, then the ripple. Each has a kill line.

## Question 1: what PIE offers for pixel work

Sources. The TRM, chapter 1 "Processor Instruction Extensions (PIE)", the copy served at documentation.espressif.com on 2026-11-02 (its version string was not in the chunks read. the page anchors are that copy's): 1.1 Overview and 1.3 Structure p. 39, 1.3.5 Address Unit p. 41, 1.5.1 Registers p. 45, SAR_BYTE p. 47, 1.5.3 Data Format and Alignment p. 48, 1.8.88 EE.VLD.128.IP p. 164. Espressif's cheat sheet in esp-dl, [esp32s3-pie-simd/SKILL.md](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl/blob/master/tools/agents/skills/esp32s3-pie-simd/SKILL.md) and its `references/pie_instruction_set.md` (version 1.0, 2026-04-02). All E unless marked.

- Registers: 8 QR registers q0 to q7, 128 bits, read as 16 x 8-bit, 8 x 16-bit or 4 x 32-bit lanes. SAR (6-bit) holds the shift after a multiply, SAR_BYTE (4-bit) the byte shift for unaligned data. ACCX is a 40-bit scalar accumulator, QACC_H and QACC_L 160-bit per-lane accumulators. Sixteen 8-bit multipliers, eight 16-bit (TRM p. 39, 45).
- Arithmetic: EE.VADDS and EE.VSUBS saturating in S8, S16, S32, with fused load (.LD.INCP) and store (.ST.INCP) forms. EE.VMUL in S8, S16, U8, U16, result shifted right by SAR, no saturation, no 32-bit multiply. EE.VMULAS multiply-accumulate into ACCX or QACC, extracted with saturation by EE.SRCMB. EE.VMAX, EE.VMIN in S8, S16, S32. EE.VCMP.EQ, LT, GT in S8 only, giving 0xFF masks for EE.ANDQ, ORQ, XORQ, NOTQ. Shifts: EE.VSL.32 and EE.VSR.32 on 32-bit lanes only, EE.SRC.Q the byte funnel shift. a 16-bit lane shift is done as EE.VMUL.U16 by a constant with SAR (D: the trick the blend and repack kernels use). EE.VZIP and VUNZIP interleave lanes (the cheat sheet's quick reference). No gather or scatter: a palette or a displacement lookup stays scalar (D, from the absence in the instruction list).
- Loads and stores: EE.VLD.128.IP and .XP, EE.VST.128.IP and .XP, 64-bit halves, broadcasts EE.VLDBC.8, 16, 32. Every access forces its low address bits to zero, 4 bits for 128-bit. So an unaligned pointer silently reads the aligned block before it (TRM p. 48). Unaligned data: EE.LD.128.USAR.IP saves the offset in SAR_BYTE, EE.VLD.128.IP loads the next block, EE.SRC.Q joins them (TRM p. 47, 48). Buffers get `__attribute__((aligned(16)))` or `heap_caps_aligned_alloc(16, ...)` (TRM p. 48). Found: the cheat sheet gives EE.VLD.128.IP's step as -512..496 and the TRM p. 164 as -2048..2032. Trust the TRM.
- Hazards: a QR load to its use costs 2 cycles, a SAR write needs one instruction gap or ISYNC (cheat sheet, "PIPELINE & SCHEDULING").
- PSRAM: no source says PIE loads from PSRAM behave differently from scalar loads. both go through the data cache (H). A trap report on esp32.com was a 128-bit load over the instruction bus, answered by Espressif: the data must be in data-accessible memory ([search result](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=104330), the thread itself was not readable: bot check). The frame is 150 KB, past the 32 KB cache, so a kernel that reads it runs at PSRAM speed whatever the instruction (E: p537-finding.md, "PSRAM". our 89 ns/px copy). Slice 0 measures a PIE load from PSRAM against internal, one number.
- Calling it from a MicroPython user C module. GCC emits no PIE instruction and does not know q0 to q7 or ACCX, so they cannot be inline-asm operands or clobbers (E: Espressif staff on esp32.com via the [search](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=146698), not read in full). Espressif's blog shows both a `.S` routine and `asm volatile` blocks whose Q registers the compiler never sees ([PIE introduction](https://developer.espressif.com/blog/2024/12/pie-introduction/), Yan Ke, 2024-12-05). its numbers are on an ESP32-P4, not ours. Every Espressif production use is a `.S` file with the windowed ABI (`entry`, args in a2 to a7, `retw`): esp-dsp's `dspi_dotprod_s16_aes3.S` (ee.vld.128.ip, ee.ld.128.usar.ip, ee.vmulas.s16.accx.ld.ip.qup, rur.accx_0, behind `#if dspi_dotprod_aes3_enabled`, with a fallback to the ansi routine for a length that is not a multiple of 8), esp_lvgl_port's `lv_color_blend_to_rgb565_esp32s3.S` (ee.movi.32.q, ee.vst.128.ip, ee.src.q with wur.sar_byte, four alignment paths, `loopnez`) and `lv_rgb565_blend_normal_to_rgb565_esp32s3.S` (an opaque copy, 16 or 24 px per iteration, no alpha). Larry Bank's minimal example is a `.S` too ([bitbank, 2024-01-31](https://bitbanksoftware.blogspot.com/2024/01/esp32-s3-simd-minimal-example.html)). Clang has `__builtin_xtensa_ee_*` intrinsics (search result, esp-rs issue 190). MicroPython builds with GCC, so not for us. Our module lists its sources in `target_sources(usermod_s3lcd INTERFACE ...)` (s3lcd/src/micropython.cmake). ESP-IDF projects enable ASM, so a `.S` added there is expected to assemble (H: esp_lvgl_port and esp-dsp build this way as IDF components. A user C module is untested, and slice 0 proves it). Flags: none special. the assembler of xtensa-esp32s3-elf accepts the `ee.*` mnemonics (E: the `.S` files above build in IDF).

## Question 2: the costs behind the table

- The one measured graphics number on PIE: esp_lvgl_port's RGB565 fill 0.196 cycles/px against 1.146 in C (5.8 x), copy 0.352 against 3.437 (9.8 x), at 128 x 128 aligned. unaligned fill gains 2.3 x (E: [simd README](https://github.com/espressif/esp-bsp/blob/master/components/esp_lvgl_port/test_apps/simd/README.md), clock and memory not stated. the ratios are mine). No published number exists for an alpha blend, a colour key, a blur or a cellular kernel on PIE. those rows are H from instruction counts.
- Our PSRAM floor: a full frame of 76,800 px copied PSRAM to PSRAM in 6.83 ms is 89 ns/px, 21 cycles. a 64 x 64 alpha blend into the frame 337.5 ns/px, 81 cycles, read-modify-write through `alpha_blend_565` (E: run46. s3lcd.c:186). So the plain C blend spends about 90 ns on PSRAM and 250 ns computing. A PIE blend spends the same 90 ns and about 15 ns computing (H: about 25 instructions per 8 px). The lesson: compute in internal RAM, write once.
- The frame budget: pace 40 ms, paint 39 to 43 ms in the scrolling games and about 16 ms in stack (E: bench.md P556). The scrolling games have no room until P554 (kept background) and P537 slices 2 and 3 land. stack has about 20 ms. So the first game is stack RENT, and every effect is a window, not a screen.
- Fire: Lode's algorithm, `fire[y][x] = (sum of below-left, below, below-right, two-below) * 32 / 129`, a random bottom row each frame, a 256-entry palette ramping red to yellow in hue and up in lightness (E: [lodev.org/cgtutor/fire.html](https://lodev.org/cgtutor/fire.html)). On PIE: three row loads, two of them through the unaligned pattern or a padded row. EE.VADDS.S8 saturates at 127, so use U16 lanes, or 8-bit lanes with a halving first. The x32/129 becomes EE.VMUL.U16 by 63 with SAR 8 (x63/256, a cooler flame, D). Smoke is the same kernel with a grey-blue palette, a slower decay and a sideways drift (D: one kernel, two palettes). The game's art rule ART-3 says soft smoke cannot be a sprite (E: council/art-direction.md:18), so this kernel is the only way to soft smoke.
- Ripples: Hugo Elias's two-buffer scheme as the Microsoft archive relays it: `Buffer2[x,y] = (Buffer1[x-1,y] + Buffer1[x+1,y] + Buffer1[x,y+1] + Buffer1[x,y-1]) / 2 - Buffer2[x,y]`, then damping, swap. render with `Xoffset = buffer[x-1,y] - buffer[x+1,y]`, `Yoffset = buffer[x,y-1] - buffer[x,y+1]`, `t = texture[x+Xoffset, y+Yoffset]` plus a shading term (E: [learn.microsoft.com archive, 2008](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/blogs/nikola/building-the-reallistic-water-ripples-sample-in-silverlight), citing Elias's page, which is offline). The damping as `x - (x >> 5)` keeps it integer (D). The texture is the background under the window. So the effect needs a snapshot at start (8 KB for 64 x 64), or the kept background of P554.
- Glow: three box blurs approximate a Gaussian within 0.04 % per pixel, and a running-sum box blur is O(n) per row regardless of radius (E: [ivank.net](https://blog.ivank.net/fastest-gaussian-blur.html)). A running sum is serial, so PIE helps a fixed 3 x 3 kernel, not a wide radius (D).
- Noise: Perlin's improved noise uses a 256-entry permutation, 12 gradients and the fade `6t^5 - 15t^4 + 10t^3` (E: [mrl.cs.nyu.edu/~perlin/noise](https://mrl.cs.nyu.edu/~perlin/noise/)). Value noise with bilinear lerp on int16 lanes is the cheaper fit here. it lost to the heat kernel on code size for the same look (D).

## Question 4: the API shape and TL-15

The game starts an effect with one call in game coordinates and forgets it:

```python
h = tft.fx(tft.FX_SMOKE, x, y, w=64, h=64, strength=200, life=30)   # a handle, or None when the slot table is full
tft.fx(tft.FX_BURST, x, y, n=24, speed=90, colour=RUST)              # particles
tft.fx(tft.FX_RIPPLE, x, y, w=64, h=64, radius=6)
tft.fx_stop(h)                                                        # early end. restore runs
tft.show()                                                            # steps every live effect by the frame time, draws it, then sends
```

- Where it sits: `fx.c` beside `frame.c`, with `frame_t` as its dependency (pixels, width, height, `frame_dirty`), no esp_lcd call, as p537's `scene.c` plan. The orientation map (lcd.py `TftCanvas`, game x, y to panel y, x) moves into the call, as P537 slice 3 plans anyway. until then `fx()` takes panel coordinates and `TftCanvas.fx()` maps them, 5 lines of Python.
- Slots: a fixed table of about 4 effects, each with its kind, window and time left. Each claims its scratch from one 16-byte aligned internal block of about 24 KB, allocated at `init()` (D: within the 31,744 B block. The probe says the exact size). No allocation per frame.
- TL-15 says "the layer draws and decides nothing" and names a particle engine in C as a direction change (E: council/tech-lead.md:60). D2 directs the effects in C. The bend proposed: an effect is a drawing that evolves in time. The C layer owns its state and clock, and the game never reads anything back: no collision, no score, no event comes from an effect. The game's speeds and rules stay in Python. So the rule becomes "draws, decides nothing the game reads" (D). The game's own `Particles` class (gfx.py:233) stays for anything the rules read. `FX_BURST` is for looks.
- Fallback: every kernel exists in plain C first (`fx_fire_c`, `fx_ripple_c`), and a `.S` twin is chosen by `#if defined(CONFIG_IDF_TARGET_ESP32S3) && FX_PIE`. The Mac tests run against the fake driver, which draws nothing for `fx()`. A board without PIE gets the C twin at the costs in the table's C column. D2 says "S3 only". the C twin costs nothing extra because slice 0 writes it as the baseline.
- Platform-neutral edge: `fx(kind, x, y, **numbers)` names no chip, no instruction and no buffer. the P539 library can carry the same signature on another core with only the C twin.

## Question 5: the sliced plan

Each slice is one PR in firebeetle-games, measured on the board, with its number in bench.md and a kill line. Clock printed (`machine.freq()`) in every run.

0. **Board probe: does PIE pay on one effect?** `s3lcd.fx_bench(op, w, h, reps)` in the module, no game change. One 64 x 64 heat kernel in plain C and in a `.S`, into a 16-byte aligned internal scratch. 200 reps, cycles per pixel from `esp_cpu_get_cycle_count()`. Also: the same kernel reading its buffer in PSRAM (the cache question), the palette map alone, and the window write into the PSRAM frame. Measures: cycles/px for C and PIE, internal and PSRAM. whether the `.S` assembles in a user C module. Pass, proposed: PIE at least 3 x over C on the internal kernel at 64 x 64. Kernel plus palette plus write under 1.5 ms for 320 x 40. Kill line: under 1.5 x, keep the C kernel, drop the `.S`, and the number stays in bench.md. About 150 lines of C and asm.
1. **Particles in C.** `FX_BURST` and `FX_DUST`: SoA int16, 64 slots, gravity, 2 x 2 or 3 x 3 dots, additive or opaque, plain C (PIE waits for a count past 1,000). `Particles.draw` keeps working for the games that read particles. Measures: update plus draw of 64 particles against today's 2.27 ms for 40. Pass: under 0.3 ms, no stale dot after the last particle dies (dirty tiles marked). Testable on the board with `probe`-style timing and by eye.
2. **Fire and smoke in stack RENT.** The heat kernel from slice 0 behind `FX_SMOKE` and `FX_FIRE` with two palettes: a dust puff at each drop (64 x 32 at the landing edge), and the tower-fall dust at game over (a 320 x 40 strip). That strip is P568's "dust" (E: council/art-direction.md:163). Measures: ms per frame with and without the effect, fps in the `time` line. Pass: D3's line, fps at most 2 below stack's own 23 to 24, the strip under 1.5 ms, and the PO's words. Kill line: over 3 ms or his "no", the strip shrinks to a window or goes.
3. **Ripple or shockwave window.** `FX_RIPPLE` 64 x 64 with a background snapshot and a restore at the end. First on the perfect drop's gold flash in stack (as the shockwave), later on HIGH WATER's surface. Measures: ms per frame, stale pixels after the restore through three drops. Pass: under 1.0 ms, no stale pixel, his words.
4. **Later, optional.** `FX_GLOW` (rank 4) on the gold flash. `FX_DISSOLVE` (rank 5) for the game-over card. Screen shake as a canvas offset (rank 6, Python, 5 lines) can ride on slice 1's PR. When a game asks, shatter as sprite-carrying particles (rank 8).

Slices 0 to 3 end with stack RENT (P568 #774) using smoke, dust particles and one ripple, measured and judged by the PO. The scrolling games take effects only after P554 and P537 slices 2 and 3 free their paint budget (E: bench.md P556, paint 39 to 43 ms at a 40 ms pace).

## What this finding does not settle

- The clock. 240 MHz is assumed. every cycle-to-ms line scales with it. Slice 0 prints it.
- Every PIE row but fill and copy is an instruction count (H), not a measurement. Slice 0 turns one of them into a number. the others follow the same ratio only as far as they resemble it.
- Whether a `.S` file assembles inside a MicroPython user C module: likely (ESP-IDF enables ASM), unproven until slice 0 builds.
- The esp32.com threads were behind a bot check. their statements come from search summaries and are marked so.
- Nothing here changes TL-15 by itself: the bend in question 4 is a proposal for the PO.
