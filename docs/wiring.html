FireBeetle 2 amplifier wiring

Ten short wires join the FireBeetle 2 ESP32-S3 (DFR0975) to the DFR0954 amplifier, with power on the breadboard rails. The 2.0 inch display stays on its GDI ribbon, and the pads it holds are marked blue. Every part is drawn to one scale, from DFRobot's drawings where they exist and from your photos where they do not. Find each pad by the name printed on your board.

The wiring guide further down draws every wire of all four parts, the rotary encoder included. It plays them one after another or step by step.

Unplug USB before you wire.

Connect the + rail to the FireBeetle 2 pad 3V3. Do not use the pad VCC, because that pad carries 5 V. In this project the + rail is 3.3 V, and the microphone must never see 5 V.

Connect the - rail to the middle ground pad, the fourth pad from the USB-C end on the 18-pin side. DFRobot's overview prints NC (not connected) on the two pads beside it.

Your photo shows the printed rail lines stop in the middle of the board. On many breadboards that gap means that each rail is two separate halves. Fit the two short bridge wires. They do no harm if the rail is continuous.

How to read the holes

  • In one column, the holes a to e are joined. The holes f to j are joined too. A wire can go in any free hole of the same half.
  • A rail runs along the board. All holes of one rail half are joined, so any hole of the rail is the same point.
  • The picture puts the FireBeetle 2 in rows b and i, and the amplifier in rows d and h. If your rows differ by one, use another free hole of that half.
  • Your breadboard prints column numbers from both ends. Do not count on the numbers. Go by the pad names.

The rotary encoder, CJMCU-111

The encoder is in the breadboard already, and two of its wires move: GA goes to the pad RX, and GB to the pad TX. Its place in the guide picture comes from your bench photo of 2026-11-10. The photo shows the blue board right of the amplifier, with its four header pins in the half with rows f to j and the knob across the middle gap. The photo does not show single holes clearly, so the encoder's columns below are from the photo, about. The pad names printed on the encoder and on the FireBeetle 2 are what count.

On 2026-11-10 the probe counted 4 edges per click with GA on SDA and GB on SCL, with no sound (run150). With the sound on, the count stood still there. On this board the bare pads SDA and SCL read high under the chip's pull-down, and a device answers on them at address 0x36 (bench.md, P757). So on 2026-11-11 the two wires moved to RX and TX in requirements.json. Nobody turned the knob on the new pads yet. Your bench uses other wire colors than this page, and that is fine.

The picture puts the pins in row f and the wires in rows g to j, so that each wire has a free hole. The microphone moved to about column 17 in the guide picture, because the encoder now sits where it was. The microphone joins no row, so its place is free.

Feed VCC from 3.3 V only, never from 5 V. If the encoder has its own pull-ups to VCC, 5 V would reach the 3.3 V pads RX and TX. Nobody has confirmed pull-ups on this board, so the firmware turns on the chip's own pull-ups. The four pads sit in one row. GA is at the end away from the lone tan capacitor, and GND is at the end beside it. The push switch of the knob is not on the header, so the firmware cannot read a press.

RX and TX belong to the board's serial console from each reset until the firmware takes them. The games take them at their start (encoder.take_pads). How long that takes after a reset is not measured, and if the start stops before that call, the console keeps both pads. Until the call the console holds TX high, and a turn of the knob can type into the console. So measure before you plug GB into TX, with the power off. Set the meter to ohms and measure from GB to GND. Turn the knob slowly between two clicks, and note the lowest number. If the meter shows OL the whole time, it measured nothing. If the lowest number is under 1 kΩ, stop: do not plug GB into TX. That limit is a choice: at 1 kΩ, a pad at 3.3 V drives 3.3 mA. The encoder's VCC is on the rail, so with the contact open the meter can show a large number in place of OL. The lowest number is the one that counts.

No dimension drawing exists for this module. Its size of about 15 x 32 mm, the knob of about 15 mm, and the places of the encoder, the hole and the small parts on it are estimated from your photo and a shop photo. Only the pad spacing of 2.54 mm is standard.

Where each fact comes from

  • FireBeetle 2 size and pad positions: DFRobot's dimension drawing V1.3, through the spark board record.
  • Which side of the FireBeetle 2 holds which row, and all connector positions: DFRobot's board overview for V1.2 and later, with the USB-C end to the right. The positions are read from that image, to about 0.5 mm. If a printed name differs from the picture, the printed name is right.
  • The two LEDs, the buttons and BAT on the FireBeetle 2: the same overview. LED colors are not drawn, because no source gives them.
  • Amplifier chip, parts and JST socket: DFRobot's dimension drawing. The drawing's parts are placed to scale.
  • Speaker size and screw holes, 70 x 30 x 16 mm and 63 x 24 mm: DFRobot's product page. The cone shape is estimated from your photo.
  • CAM is DFRobot's name for the DVP camera connector. It stays empty.
  • The ribbon route follows your photo: it leaves the GDI connector toward USB-C, turns back, and enters the display at its short end.
  • The amplifier has two VCC pads and two GND pads. This wiring uses the pair on the SD side. The amplifier's schematic is not in the store, so nobody confirmed that each pair is joined. A meter confirms it in one touch.
  • Amplifier size and pad positions: DFRobot's drawing and DXF file. Your photo shows the same pin names in the same order.
  • Pad names on the FireBeetle 2 side of the wires: D10, D11, D12 and A2 are GPIO 14, 13, 12 and 6 in the record. If the tone stays silent, suspect D10 first.
  • Rail colors and the gap in the middle: your photos. Nobody measured whether the gap breaks the rail.
  • Display pins: games/gdi.py and the DFR0664 part record, read from the board schematic.
  • Microphone pads and their order: the spark part record inmp441-i2s-mic-round, read from your photo of the pad side. Its wires are fixed in requirements.json: SCK on A3, WS on A5 and SD on A4. D14 stays free, because it is the user button that the games read.
  • Encoder pads, their order and the 3.3 V rule: the spark part record cjmcu-111-rotary-encoder. One fact in that record is measured on the bench: 4 edges per click (run150). The other facts are still marked unverified.
  • Encoder wires: spark's breadboard sheet for P758, build/breadboard.md, steps 5, 7, 23, 28 and 29. It joins GA to RX (GPIO 44) and GB to TX (GPIO 43), as requirements.json asks since 2026-11-11.
  • Encoder shape and color: your photo and the shop photo in the drawer entry cjmcu-111. No dimension drawing exists, so its sizes are estimates.
  • Encoder place: your bench photo of 2026-11-10. The columns are read from it, about, and no single hole is clear in it.
  • Encoder wiring checked: not yet on RX and TX. On the old pads SDA and SCL the probe counted 4 edges per click (run150). With no wire, the pulse counter counted on RX and TX (run171), and the games took both pads at their start (run175).
