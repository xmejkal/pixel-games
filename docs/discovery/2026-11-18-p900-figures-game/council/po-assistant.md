# PO assistant: P900, figures game (2026-11-18)

Lens: existing first, risks and assumptions, pre-mortem, overlaps and order. Nothing changed outside this file. council/tools-survey.md did not exist when I read the folder, so I cite nothing from it.

## Findings

Existing: what in the product already does this

- PO-1 · Nothing in the product lets one player switch between figures. Each game has one steered figure: Rescue's `Fireman` (`games/rescue.py:90`) and Meteors' walker inside `Rules`. The people in Rescue are steered by a rule, with no player pick (`Person.run`, `games/rescue.py:217-229`). · E `games/rescue.py:90,203-229`; `games/meteors.py:1-12` · supports: job 2 has no workaround today
- PO-2 · The part that "steers a figure from outside" already exists in three pieces: `ActionMap` (inputs to actions, drivers handed in from outside, "so a remote board's map can bind other inputs (P832)"), `SubPixel` (a speed in px/s stepped by frame ms, rest kept) and `Chooser` (reference vs fast version). So P901's steering seam has real precedent, and the stick already steers the fireman through `Fireman.steer(ms, way, held)` and `lean`. · E `games/engine/README.md:8-25`; `games/rescue.py:128-140`; `games/meteors.py:27-28` · supports: P901 is a small extraction, not a new invention
- PO-3 · The figure's own state is repeated by hand, as the card says. `Fireman` and `Person` each hold x, facing, `rest` (the SubPixel remainder), `ran` and a pose computed as `ran * POSES // STRIDE_PX % POSES`. The numbers (`RUN_POSES`, `STRIDE_PX`, `PERSON_STRIDE_PX`) are typed in from what `art/mocap.py` prints. · E `games/rescue.py:39-43,60-62,96-101,178-182,207-215`; `art/mocap.py` docstring (run, spray, turn, carry, CROWD) · supports: job 4; the extraction target is two classes in one file plus one walker in a second file. The other thirteen games were not read (E, scope of this lens).
- PO-4 · The mocap pipeline already yields what a "pose" part needs: each pose is a dict of joint to (forward, up, near) in body heights, floor at up 0, hips at forward 0. The art is painted offline into pictures; the board never sees skeleton data. So "work with the poses" on the board means choosing a picture by phase, not skeleton maths. · E `art/mocap.py:1-30`; `games/rescue.py:178-182` · supports: a poses part on the board is a phase-to-picture lookup unless the PO wants skeleton work on the board (not asked)
- PO-5 · At 96 px the art does not fit the way it does now. Today a Rescue figure is a 26x30 picture (run), 40x30 (spray), 30x30 (carry), 24x32 (man run), stored per facing, 2 bytes a pixel, 355 pictures in a 659,992-byte pack. Rescue's run/spray/turn/carry cycles alone, both facings, are 60,960 px x 2 B = 121,920 B; at 4 times the linear size (16 times the area) that is about 1.95 MB for the fireman's four cycles, about 0.98 MB if one facing is mirrored in code. The picture pool is made as big as the largest pack today, 739,216 B (runner). The five people's cycles (man, woman, dogman, granny, child) are 36,864+36,864+36,864+32,256+19,008 = 161,856 px; at 4x linear, both facings stored, that is 161,856 x 2 x 16 = about 5.2 MB. · E `games/art_index.py:92` (`rescue_run_r0` is 26 x 30); a count of the pictures in `games/art_index.py` with `rescue` set (355, sum of w x h x 2 = 659,992 B, my command); `README.md:40` (pool, 739,216 B); the 8 MB PSRAM in `bench.md:43` · caveat: the 4x figures are my arithmetic, not a measurement · supports: RAM and pack size are the first feasibility gate (the brief's first open question), and the brief's pass line must say how many cycles and figures per level
- PO-6 · The frame cost of a bigger figure is probably not the blit. Measured: a full 320x240 keyed blit from PSRAM took 2.65 ms (P562), and a keyed 16x24 sprite 19.8 us. A 104 x 120 figure is 12,480 px, 16% of a full frame; scaling gives about 0.4 ms (H, my scaling, not measured). What costs is the send: windows past 40 make the driver send the whole frame, 31 ms at the real 40 MHz. Big moving figures touch many 16 px tiles each frame, so two or three of them may pass 40 windows. · E `bench.md:100,167-171,86`; H arithmetic · supports: the test of the art budget has to run on the board with the real number of tiles, not on the Mac
- PO-7 · Today's frame rates leave little room. Rescue idle runs 18 to 20 fps with a worst gap of 84 to 150 ms, Meteors 16 to 21. The pace is 40 ms. A new game with a figure 16 times larger starts from a game that is already below its pace. · E `bench.md:1646-1656` (P846 baseline) · supports: a frame-time spike before design (the brief names it as open, and it is the cheapest feasibility test)
- PO-8 · The engine plan's rules already fix how each new part must look: one interface, a plain Python reference, a fast twin chosen once at boot by `accel.Chooser`, a twin test per part (`tests/test_twins.py` fails for a part without one), and a boundary test on imports. The plan's stages 1 (input) and 0b-2 (choice) are partly done (README lists `actions.py`, `accel.py`, `subpixel.py`); stages 3 to 9 are not. · E `games/engine/README.md:8-37`; `docs/discovery/2026-11-16-p844-engine-plan/plan.md:228-248` · supports: P901 can start on what exists (actions, accel, subpixel) without waiting for stages 3 to 8

Outside: which small published games do this

- PO-12 · The Lost Vikings (1992, Silicon and Synapse, now Blizzard): one player, three Vikings with different abilities, switched at any time; the goal is all three at the exit; Blizzard's own retrospective says the stages cannot be crossed without the Vikings supporting each other; its concept was inspired by Lemmings. Idle Vikings: not stated in what I could read; one source says an auto-follow to the exit was planned, which hints they were passive. The smallest mission it teaches (H, model knowledge, not read in a source): one Viking opens or clears a way that the one with the missing ability needs, then both arrive. Licence: proprietary game, its art and code cannot be used (H). · E https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23609765/the-lost-vikings-and-how-we-learned-to-love-multiplayer-puzzles and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Vikings, search result text read 2026-11-18 (not fetched in full) · supports: the closest prior art for D1; it does not give a verified answer for idle figures
- PO-13 · Trine (2009): one player switches between three characters (thief, knight, wizard); only one body exists in the world at a time, and a switch puts the new one at the old one's place; each has its own life meter, and a dead one is unusable until the next checkpoint. So an idle figure does not exist at all in Trine: it has no life of its own. Licence: proprietary (H). · E https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Trine_(video_game) and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trine_2 (search result text, 2026-11-18) · supports: the opposite design to D4 (no idle figure, hence no AI)
- PO-14 · Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons (2013): one player steers two brothers at once, each by one stick, with one trigger each for the action. No switching and no idle figure. The Switch version also allows two players. Needs two sticks and two triggers; the box has one stick and one button plus a knob. Licence: proprietary (H). · E https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2013/08/06/games-brothers-a-tale-of-two-sons-feature/ and https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/brothers_a_tale_of_two_sons (search result text, 2026-11-18) · supports: it does not fit the box's input, so only D1's switching has prior art, not "both at once"
- PO-15 · Lemmings: the player does not steer the figures, they walk by themselves and the player gives a role to one at a time. That is the idle-figure answer "the box steers them by a simple rule", and it is the case where D4's AI is nothing but a fixed walk rule with a bounce at obstacles. The Lemmings clone Pingus is free software under GPLv3 or later (from GPL-2.0-or-later since July 2008), so its code cannot be copied into spark (MIT); its ideas can. · E https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingus and https://github.com/Pingus/pingus (search result text, 2026-11-18); the licence conflict with MIT is H, my reading · supports: Rescue's `Person` (a rule in `rules.py` terms: run, turn at an end, turn from a fire, wave when trapped) is already this class of "simple AI"; `games/rescue.py:203-229, 311-343`
- PO-16 · No open-source "Lost Vikings" clone turned up in the search; I did not search further. Gobliiins (1991) is named as an earlier game with character swapping. · E search result text, same page, 2026-11-18 · supports: the reuse answer for the mission part is "design from published descriptions, no code"
- PO-17 · About D4's "the s3's AI capabilities": the S3 has 128-bit vector instructions on the LX7 core for neural-network work, and Espressif's library ESP-DL runs quantised models (w8a8, w16a16, w8a16) in the .espdl format. The results named no MicroPython binding and no game character use. A figure's own life as a state machine needs none of this. The chip fact is E; whether a neural network would add anything over a state machine for a figure is an unknown. · E https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/esp-dl/versions/3.3.6/readme and https://developer.espressif.com/workshops/edge-ai-with-esp32-s3/introduction/ (search result text, 2026-11-18); `#1279` comment ("a wish, not a checked fact", brief D4) · supports: the AI's technique question (open in the brief) can start with the state machine that Rescue already has

Risks and assumptions

- PO-18 · Is anyone blocked without this now? No. The only user is the PO (n = 1); the figures work as 24 px figures; he said they are "tiny" and "one doesn't appreciate the details so much" (the card). "so I can sell it more" is his stated reason (job 1). The brief says the visitor is H. · E `#1279` body; brief.md "Who" · supports: nothing is blocked, so a bad answer costs only the PO's time and the lanes' limits (board 4 of 4 in flight, brief board actions)
- PO-19 · What must be true for P900 to work, in the order I would test them. A1: a 96 px figure with enough poses to look like motion capture fits the art memory and the frame time of the board (PO-5, PO-6, PO-7). A2: one stick, one button and one knob can pick and switch among figures without the PO losing the figure he steers (usability; H, no test). A3: a collaboration task exists that is short, readable on a 320x240 screen at 96 px (about 40% of the screen height; two figures take about a third of the width) and not a puzzle that needs text (feasibility and value; H). A4: an unheld figure's own life reads as alive and does not wreck the mission (it walks off, it dies, it blocks). A5: a plain-state-machine AI for an idle figure is enough, so no neural net is needed (D4's wish). A6: the same steering part can steer a non-character (D6) with no change; only Rescue's meteors and Meteors' balls are non-characters today, and they are not steered by the same kind of rule. A7: the PO will play a mission more than once; one mission may show the parts but not the game. · E/H as tagged · supports: the order of tests
- PO-20 · Riskiest belief (proposal, the decider rates it): A1, "a 96 px motion-capture figure with a convincing cycle fits in the pool and the frame budget beside a second figure and a background". It is the one that can kill the whole item before any design work, and its test is cheap: one board run that loads one 96 px run cycle (12 poses, one facing) as pictures, blits one or two figures over Rescue's night scene, and logs fps, the slowest gap and the window count. Pass line to be dated before any result (not set by me). I judge A1 riskiest on the numbers in PO-5 and PO-7; A2 is the riskiest on use, and its test needs the first mission, so it comes second. · E arithmetic in PO-5, bench numbers in PO-7; H the ranking · supports: Q1
- PO-21 · Pre-mortem (after Klein), "six months after this shipped, and it failed". Stated failures, each H unless a pointer is given. (a) Art memory: one fireman at 96 px takes 1 to 2 MB, a mission needs two to four figure types, the pool or the load time (Rescue's 548 ms for 660 KB, README:383) becomes seconds, and the PO stops playing it; the PO's own P822 shows he dislikes a stopped screen at a switch (up to 0.6 s). (b) The stutter: a frame already at 18 to 20 fps drops below 15 with 96 px figures, which a feel slice (stops at "ready to play") will show at once. (c) The pick is clumsy: with one stick for walking, the switch costs a button or the knob, and he walks the wrong figure; the mission becomes about the controls. (d) The mission is the same each time: "there is some collaboration mission always" needs more than one, and a mission needs level design, which is the part that cannot be generated by motion capture or by the engine; one mission gets played twice and the game ends. (e) The idle figure is a bug farm: a figure that walks by itself into a fire or off the screen ends the mission before the player can act; Rescue's own first play ended with "I dont get the rescue game, it doesnt work" (E `#1124` comment 2026-11-13) because there was no readable goal. (f) The reusable parts are shaped by one game: the figure part keeps Rescue's `ran`/`STRIDE_PX` assumptions, and the car (P902) or the remote player (D7) finds that "a steered thing" has other state; P903 then costs more than the game. (g) The refactor hits the golden net: moving Rescue or Meteors onto the parts changes a hash, and the net's own rule makes each changed hash its own PR. (h) The cost of a new game on a single bench: every feel slice waits for the PO to play, and the PO's bench time is the queue. (i) The AI wish: "the S3's AI" turns into a spike that finds no MicroPython binding and the card waits. · E (e) `#1124` comment, 2026-11-13; (a) `README.md:383`, `#1175` body; (b) PO-7 · supports: the order of tests, and nothing is decided here
- PO-22 · Not blocked, but a limit applies: spark's rules file puts the lanes' limits in the hook (W6). The brief says Discovery holds 2 of 2 and the board 4 of 4 in flight (brief board actions), so P901 cannot enter Ready beside P900 unless a card leaves. · E brief.md "Board actions"; the rules text is in CLAUDE.local.md (W6), I did not read the hook · supports: Q3
