Where the PO stands today (the product, a benchmark, an account):
- [★C1] Run 22 at FRAME_MS 40: flappy 21 to 24 fps, runner 20 to 21 fps, stack 24. Runner's paint is 34 ms a frame, the worst gap 70 to 80 ms. The paint sets the rate now, not the send · E `council/user.md` USER-2, bench.md:116 (3 games, one board, one session, 2026-11-02) · caveat: the per-phase numbers are maxima per 5 s interval, not averages <!-- REQUIRED:findings -->
- [★C2] The PO's verdict on run 22 was "I saw it, looks great". The defect he names unprompted is tearing and "steps", not fps: "pipes are still half rendered" (09:10), "still appear a bit stepped" (11:00). He has no number for the step he accepts · E `council/user.md` USER-1, USER-3, USER-4, bench.md:76, bench.md:117 · caveat: an account of one person on one day, n = 1
- [★C3] The paint is Python call overhead and repeated work, not pixel work. A full-frame RGB565 fill in C costs 0.37 ms at 240 MHz and a full-frame copy 1.10 ms, against 34 ms of paint. PIE would cut those to 0.06 and 0.11 ms · E `council/tech-lead.md` TL-7, PHYS-9, `council/chip-hardware.md` HW-1, HW-S3-9, build-s3lcd/sdkconfig:1592 · caveat: cycles per pixel are Espressif's measurement on an S3 against LVGL's ANSI C, applied by arithmetic, "call overhead" is H from phase maxima
- [★C4] P537 projects that about 140 C lines inside the s3lcd fork (a kept background and glyph text) bring runner's paint from 34 ms to about 8 ms. J1, smooth games, needs no public multi-chip library, only P537 slices 0 to 3 · E `council/po-assistant.md` PO-1, p537-finding.md options table, rank 2 · caveat: a projection from per-interval maxima, H until slice 0 measures the averages
- [★C5] A full frame at 40 MHz needs 30.72 ms on the wire (calculated), 31.0 ms measured. 40 MHz is the highest clock this panel takes: 80 MHz gave a dark screen, and the S3 SPI clock is an integer divider · E `council/tech-lead.md` PHYS-1, bench.md:38, bench.md:84 · caveat: the 80 MHz source clock is H, the IDF page gives no value
- [★C6] A window costs about 128 µs of fixed overhead, fitted on three measured sends (126.8, 128.7, 129.5 µs). At windows=40 that is 5.1 ms, a sixth of a full frame. `frame.c:29` says 60 to 120 µs · E `council/tech-lead.md` PHYS-2, s3lcd/src/s3lcd.c:2809, bench.md:77, bench.md:100 · caveat: fitted on runs that carried the one-row strip bug, so a board probe with n windows of equal bytes must settle it
- [★C7] Internal DMA-capable RAM holds one frame, not two: the largest free block is 188,416 B, a frame 153,600 B, so 34,816 B of that block remain. A double buffer on the S3 at this resolution is PSRAM or nothing · E `council/tech-lead.md` PHYS-4, bench.md slice 0 (09:58) · caveat: the largest block after `init()` was not measured
- [★C8] 60 fps is out of reach for any frame that changes more than half the screen on this bus: at most 83,500 B go out per 16.7 ms, 54 % of a frame. Game play changes 22 to 31 % of the frame (flappy 14 windows 11.3 ms, runner 7 windows 7.7 ms) · E `council/tech-lead.md` PHYS-3, PHYS-6 (calculated from bench.md probe6) · caveat: reachable for play only if paint and send overlap
- [★C9] On IDF v5.5.2 the SPI DMA cannot read PSRAM: the driver re-allocates a PSRAM buffer in internal RAM and copies it. IDF master adds `SPI_TRANS_DMA_USE_PSRAM`. Forum numbers: PSRAM to internal about 50 MB/s, internal memcpy about 361 MB/s · E `council/chip-hardware.md` HW-S3-21, HW-S3-22, `council/tech-lead.md` PHYS-7 · caveat: the bandwidths are field numbers from forum posts, not measured here

Toolchains, languages and the cost of C++ (documents, a benchmark):

- [★C16] ESP-IDF 5.5.2 uses GCC 14.2.0 with `-std=gnu17` and `-std=gnu++2b`, exceptions and RTTI off. arduino-pico 6.3.2 uses gnu23 and gnu++23. The Pico SDK sets no standard.
