Two findings behind the table:

1. **The "55.3 ns/px write" is a difference, not a cost.** It is the extra time a memcpy takes into a PSRAM destination over an internal one (E: bench.md:198, both rows are the same `blit_buffer` memcpy path, s3lcd.c:614). The brief's ★C3 says "a write adds 55.3 ns/px", which is right. Then ★C6 ("one write a pixel: about 4.25 ms"), ★C15 ("writes opaque pixels at about 55 ns/px") and every PHYS-5 row use 55.3 as the whole cost of a C loop writing computed pixels into the PSRAM frame. No measurement in bench.md gives that cost. The only measured whole costs are 88.9 ns/px (PSRAM to PSRAM memcpy) and 33.6 ns/px (PSRAM to internal memcpy). A kernel that computes a pixel and writes it, in panel order, with no source read from PSRAM, costs somewhere between those and the delta, and it depends on the cache's write-back behaviour, which nobody measured. P580 made the same substitution ("PSRAM write 55 ns/px", p580-finding.md:15). Every full-screen cost in the brief is then up to about 1.6 x higher (88.9 / 55.3) before any kernel cycle is counted. The sentence to change in ★C3's caveat: add "55.3 is the PSRAM penalty over an internal memcpy, not the cost of a write. The cost of a computed write into the PSRAM frame is unmeasured."
2. **The 15 ms room against 25 ms (★C4, A4, Q10) is settled by a log the folder has and did not read.** run55-p593-rain-c160.log holds five `time` and `avg` lines (E, one of them): `time rain step 1.7 paint 29.9 send 3.2 gc 1.7 fps 24` and `avg rain static 4.7 paint 14.2 fx 0.1 hud 10.8`. So per frame at N 160: static background fill 4.6 to 4.7 ms, the rain's draw 13.6 to 14.2 (the brief's "draw"), HUD 10.6 to 10.9. Then the step wrapper 1.7 to 1.8 (engine step 0.17 plus read 1.1 plus Python), the `show()` copy 3.2 to 3.3, gc 1.6 to 1.7. Sum 36.2 to 36.7 ms. The frame printed 24 fps, so it averaged 40.0 to 41.7 ms, which puts the loop and pace at 3.5 to 5 ms. At N 192 the sum is 39.2 to 40.3 ms and the frame printed 23 (E: run54-p593-rain-c.log). Consequences:
   - The tech lead's "14 ms rest, inferred, not measured per part" is wrong in its caveat: the parts are measured (static 4.6, send 3.2, gc 1.6) and sum to 9.5. The unmeasured loop is 4 to 5 ms, not 14.
   - The room for an effect's step plus draw, when it replaces the rain's step, read and draw (15.2 ms) and keeps everything else: about 15.2 + (40 − 36.5) = 18.7 ms for a 40 ms frame. Up to 20.4 ms for a frame that still prints 24. Neither 15 nor 25.
   - The brief never names the lever that changes this most: `lcd.py:142` skips the background fill when `game.covers` is true (E: `if not game.covers: tft.fill(game.bg)`. gfx.py:286 defines `covers`). An opaque full-screen effect covers the screen, so it can drop the 4.6 ms fill. Then the room is about 23 to 25 ms with today's HUD, and the PO assistant's 25 ms is close for that case. Q10's premise, that only a cached HUD doubles the room, is incomplete: the cheaper lever is already in the code.
   - A4's pass line ("step plus draw plus HUD at 24 fps sits within 2 ms of 26 ms") measures nothing: the sum of three parts at a fps the pace fixes is not a room. The room is 40 ms minus the measured rest, and the rest is in the log today.
