## Findings

### Existing first: in this repository

- PO-1 · P580 already ranks eight effects with a cost each. In rank order: fire and smoke from a heat kernel, particles, ripple and shockwave, glow, dissolve, screen shake, noise smoke, shatter. None of the eight is colliding bodies, burning bodies or falling or pouring water. A ripple seen from above is its only water · E p580-finding.md:17-28 · supports: three of the PO's words (fire, smoke, part of "water") have a costed method already. The other three (colliding, on fire, water as a liquid) have none.
- PO-2 · Most P580 costs are estimates. Two compose figures are measured: the C alpha blend at 337.5 ns/px into the PSRAM frame, and the PSRAM write at 55 ns/px. The kernel cycles and every PIE row are instruction counts (H). The refuter showed that one measured number, the compose, moved rank 1 by 4 times · E p580-finding.md:96, p580-refuter.md:149-151 · supports: the costs of the ten scenes are not known before they run. Only the compose is.
- PO-3 · The C engine has no contact between bodies. Each body falls onto its own plane `rest_y`, bounces on it and goes to sleep. world.c holds no pair test, no shape and no radius. The rain gives each body a random plane (`H - SIZE - random.randrange(40)`) · E engine/core/world.h:30-60, engine/core/world.c:62-80, games/rain.py:120-123 · supports: "colliding bodies" needs new contact code, the same kind of code as P592's rigid tip and D16's own solver. It is not a variant of what exists.
- PO-4 · The engine module has no drawing path. Its binding exports load, step, read_y, spawn, read, drain, call_back and nothing, with no s3lcd or frame symbol. The rain draws each body from Python with `cv.fill`. 160 fills of 4 x 4 px cost 13.5 to 14.0 ms a frame (measured), about 85 µs per fill (calculated). The step costs 0.16 to 0.17 ms · E engine/binding.c:141-151, games/rain.py:66-70, bench.md P593 table · supports: an effect that Python draws pixel by pixel or blob by blob is draw-bound, as the rain is. A field effect (heat, wave) needs C to write the pixels. There are two ways: C fills a buffer that Python blits with the existing `blit_buffer`, or C writes into the frame through s3lcd.h (D17). Neither path exists in the engine module today.
- PO-5 · The rain's HUD text costs 10.6 to 10.9 ms a frame, more than the plan's guess of 3 to 8 ms. The pace is 40 ms, so fps tops out at 25, and the rain sits at 24 · E bench.md P593 notes, bench.md P556 (pace 40 ms) · supports: a scene built on the rain's template has about 40 - 11 = 29 ms for the effect, its compose and the frame copy (calculated). The copy of a full frame is 3.4 ms (E: run41 via p580-finding.md:24), which leaves about 25 ms (calculated). fps cannot show headroom below the cap, so a scene must show its cost in ms, as the rain's step bar does.
- PO-6 · The games' own effects today are all particle bursts or offsets, never a field. `Particles` holds at most 40 particles. Each is a Python list `[x, y, vx, vy, life, colour, gravity]`. A `@native` loop steps them, and each is drawn as a 2 or 3 px fill. `die()` fires two bursts and `pop()` fires one. Other uses:
  - rising sparkles with negative gravity (arcade.py:175)
  - landing dust (arcade.py:143, arcade2.py:73)
  - the perfect-drop burst (arcade2.py:95-96)
  - the death shake `SHAKE = (3, -2, 2, -1)` (arcade2.py:27)
  - the collapse dust (arcade2.py:168) and smoke puffs (arcade2.py:237)
  - a spark trail (world3d.py:40) and a flash timer (space3d.py:126-176).

  40 particles cost 2.27 ms per update in Python (run46) · E games/gfx.py:225-258, 311-321, the lines named, p580-finding.md:20 · supports: sparks, dust and debris already exist as a look. So a C particle scene gains speed and count, not a new look. Fire, smoke, water and collisions are new looks.
- PO-7 · The rain passes every feel number from Python: GRAVITY 2000, RESTITUTION 0.35, FRICTION 0.6 and the spawn ranges. C only steps · E games/rain.py:28-31, 120-123, D12 (the PO, 2026-11-03, engine brief) · supports: the pattern the effect scenes can follow. Each tuning number of an effect is a feel number under D12: cooling, sparking, spread, damping, palette. P580 makes the same point for gravity and drag (p580-finding.md:77).
- PO-8 · A scene tried alone on black needs no alpha compose. The effect owns its pixels, so it writes opaque at about 55 ns/px. In a game it reads the background, blends and writes. That costs 337.5 ns/px in C, about 6 times as much (calculated from the two E figures in PO-2). A full screen of 76,800 px costs about 4.2 ms opaque and about 25.9 ms blended in C (calculated) · E p580-finding.md:15, 44 · supports: a scene's measured cost alone understates its cost in a game, unless the scene also draws over a picture. This is the gap between P594 (alone) and P583 (in RENT).
- PO-9 · Internal RAM for effects is tight. The largest free block while the games run is 31,744 B (run41). The engine's two worlds take 12,320 B of static RAM at 256 bodies. A 64 x 64 ripple takes 24,576 B, and P580's proposed arena is about 18.4 KB · E bench.md P556 via p580-finding.md:15, engine/core/world.h:9 comment, p580-finding.md:21, 76 · caveat: free internal RAM on the engine image was not measured (bench.md P593: "Free internal RAM at start was not measured") · supports: ten scenes can each fit alone. A full-screen 8-bit field (76,800 B) or an RGB565 window larger than about 100 x 100 px (20 KB, calculated) goes to PSRAM, where P580 prices reads at 34 ns/px and writes at 55 ns/px.

### Existing first: outside (licences checked through the GitHub API on 2026-11-03)

- PO-10 · FastLED is MIT (gh api `repos/FastLED/FastLED`: `MIT`, 7,523 stars). Its effects: in `src/fl/fx/1d` cylon, demoreel100, fire2012, noisewave, pacifica (an ocean look), particles, perlin_particle_punch, pride2015, twinklefox. In `src/fl/fx/2d` animartrix, blend, flowfield, luminova, noisepalette, redsquare, scale_up, wave ("2D wave simulation effect for LED matrices ... realistic water-like wave effects", wave.h:1-5). Fire2012 (Mark Kriegsman, July 2012) is one-dimensional: a heat cell array where each cell cools, heat drifts up and diffuses, random sparks enter at the bottom, and heat maps to colour through a black-body palette. COOLING defaults to 55 ("suggested range 20-100") and SPARKING to 120 ("50-200"), at a suggested 30 to 100 fps · E gh api `contents/src/fl/fx/1d/fire2012.h` lines 1-50, `contents/src/fl/fx/2d/wave.h` · supports: MIT allows copying with the notice kept. The code is written for LED strips and matrices of a few hundred pixels in CRGB 24-bit, not RGB565 frames of 76,800 px, so a port is a rewrite in any case (H: from the CRGB types in the headers).
- PO-11 · WLED is EUPL-1.2 (gh api `repos/wled/WLED`: `EUPL-1.2`, 18,961 stars). FX.cpp: "Licensed under the EUPL v. 1.2 or later. Adapted from code originally licensed under the MIT license". FXparticleSystem.cpp: "Copyright (c) 2024 Damian Schneider. Licensed under the EUPL v. 1.2 or later". It holds 25 particle-system effects, among them PS Fire, PS Ballpit, PS Waterfall, PS Volcano, PS Impact, PS Fireworks, PS Box, PS Hourglass, PS DripDrop, PS Sparkler, PS Vortex and PS Sonic Boom. Its 2D effects include Firenoise, Drift, Metaballs, Game Of Life, Plasma Ball, Polar Lights, Soap, Distortion Waves and Rotozoomer · E gh api `contents/wled00/FX.cpp` (11,310 lines, names grepped), `contents/wled00/FXparticleSystem.cpp` lines 1-10 · H: EUPL-1.2 is a copyleft licence, so its code cannot enter an MIT repository without the whole file falling under EUPL (model knowledge; the EUPL text was not read) · supports: WLED serves as a menu of what LED-matrix authors found worth building. Fire, colliding balls, waterfall, volcano and impact are all on it. It is a source of ideas only. An agent told "feel free to get inspired by other code" must not paste from it.
- PO-12 · Adafruit_PixelDust is "particle simulation for 'LED sand' (or dust, or snow or rain or whatever)" by Phil Burgess. Its grains move in an integer space 256 times finer than the pixel grid and collide with each other and with obstacles on a bitmap. Its licence line reads "BSD license, all text here must be included in any redistribution", and GitHub detects no SPDX licence · E gh api `contents/Adafruit_PixelDust.h` lines 1-40, `repos/adafruit/Adafruit_PixelDust` (licence field empty, 91 stars) · — which BSD variant, 2-clause or 3-clause, was not checked · supports: the closest existing thing to "many colliding bodies" and "sand or liquid" on a microcontroller. BSD is compatible with MIT if the notice is kept.
- PO-13 · Pimoroni's pimoroni-pico is MIT (gh api, 1,566 stars). Its fire examples (galactic_unicorn/fire_effect.cpp, cosmic, stellar, interstate75, plasma_stick, MicroPython vertical-fire.py) use a float heat buffer of about 53 x 11 cells and pick colour by thresholds (`heat > 0.5` → (255, 255, 180), and so on). balls.cpp, despite its name, is the same fire · E gh api `contents/examples/galactic_unicorn/fire_effect.cpp`, `balls.cpp` lines 1-40, code search `fire repo:pimoroni/pimoroni-pico` · supports: it adds nothing beyond Lode's and Kriegsman's kernels. It is evidence that the heat kernel is the standard fire on small matrices.
- PO-14 · Spring water seen from the side: Michael Hoffman's tutorial of 2012-08-14 models the surface as a row of vertical springs, `a = -k/m x - d v` with k 0.025 and damping 0.025. It spreads to neighbours with `leftDeltas[i] = Spread * (h[i] - h[i-1])`, Spread from 0 to 0.5, in 8 passes. A splash sets one spring's speed, and spray particles spawn when the speed is over 60, `speed / 8` of them. The article states no licence for its code · E WebFetch of code.tutsplus.com/make-a-splash-with-dynamic-2d-water-effects--gamedev-236t, read 2026-11-03 · H: one column per game pixel, 240 or 320 springs x 8 passes is a few thousand operations a frame, under 0.1 ms in C (calculated from P589's 0.135 µs per float body-step as a rough scale) · supports: the cheapest "water" that a body can fall into. P583's RENT or HIGH WATER can reuse it later. Reimplement it from the formula and copy no code.
- PO-15 · Falling-sand automata on microcontrollers exist: a digital hourglass on an ESP32 with a 128 x 64 OLED that keeps the grid as a bit-packed array with per-pixel swap rules, and LED hourglass kits on 8 x 8 matrices · H: from the search summary only. hackaday.io/project/165620 and the Arduino blog were not opened · supports: a grid of grains, sand and water cells, is a known cheap effect on small chips. Water in such grids is a cell that also moves sideways (H, same search).
- PO-16 · No fire demo for an ESP32 with an ST7789 TFT turned up in one search. P580 found no ESP32-S3 effect demo on PIE. TFT_eSPI reports its licence as NOASSERTION (gh api) · E WebSearch "ESP32 ST7789 TFT fire effect demo github", 2026-11-03; p580-finding.md:61 · supports: there is nothing on our panel and chip to take whole. Every scene is our own code, learned from MIT, BSD or formula-only sources.
- PO-17 · esp-dsp is Apache-2.0 and holds no pixel kernel. It offers the `.S` with an ANSI fallback pattern · E gh api (Apache-2.0), p580-finding.md:56 · — whether its 2D convolution or image functions fit an RGB565 blur was not checked · supports: "through ESP-IDF" in the PO's words gives the build path for `.S` files, not ready effects.

### Question 2: ten effects, ranked by "cool" per unit of work

The kernels behind them (my grouping, H):
- K1, a heat field: Lode, Fire2012. P580 rank 1.
- K2, C particles in structure-of-arrays form. P580 rank 2.
- K3, a 2D wave from above: Elias. P580 rank 3.
- K4, a 1D spring surface: Hoffman (PO-14).
- K5, circle contacts between bodies (new, PO-3).
- K6, a grain grid: falling sand, PixelDust-like (PO-12, PO-15).

- PO-18 · Proposed ranking (opinion, the PO assistant). Rank 1 is the most "cool" per unit of work. Each line names its kernel:
  1. Fire (K1). The PO's first word. The kernel is known (Lode, Fire2012, Pimoroni) and P580 costs it at 1.9 ms for a 64 x 64 window in C, blended. Alone and opaque, it costs less (PO-8).
  2. Smoke (K1). The same kernel with a grey palette, slower cooling and a sideways drift (p580-finding.md:46). Almost no new code.
  3. Bodies on fire (K1 with the rain's world). The rain's falling bodies inject heat at their positions, so each trails flame as it falls. It reuses world.c, `engine.read` and the heat kernel. It is the first scene where the C engine and an effect meet, which the epic P586 needs later. This depends on the meaning asked in Q2 of the questions below.
  4. Water with splashes, side view (K4 plus K2). Bodies drop into a spring surface, the surface waves, and droplets fly. The PO's word "water", in the cheapest form that reacts to bodies.
  5. Sparks, fireworks or an explosion (K2). Sparks already exist in Python (PO-6), so what is new is the count (hundreds instead of 40) and the speed. P583 needs this kernel anyway.
  6. Colliding bodies (K5). Round bodies in a box knock each other about, a ball pit. The PO's word. The most new code of the ten, and it overlaps P592 and D16 (PO-3).
  7. Ripple on water from above (K3). P580 rank 3. A second reading of "water". It needs a picture under it to bend, or it shows only shading.
  8. Falling sand and liquid grains (K6). Grains pile, flow and collide. A cheap form of "colliding bodies" and "water" at once, and very interactive. "And more".
  9. Shockwave (K3 as one ring) or glow (P580 rank 4). Cheap once the ripple or a blur exists.
  10. Plasma or clouds (value noise, P580 rank 7). A classic full-screen look of the demoscene at an opaque write cost of about 4.2 ms (calculated, PO-8). Or dissolve (P580 rank 5). A pick for the PO.

  Cheap variants of one kernel: fire, smoke and bodies on fire share K1. Sparks, fireworks, splash droplets and embers share K2. Ripple and shockwave share K3. The ten scenes are about six kernels. K5 and K6 are the only two with no prior costing · H: my grouping, built on P580's table and PO-10 to PO-15.
- PO-19 · Which kernels serve later work: K1 and K2 are P583's "particles (dust, debris, sparks) and smoke from the heat kernel" in its done-when, and K3 is P580's slice 3 for HIGH WATER. K5 is the only one that serves the engine's solver (P592, D16) rather than effects · E #784 "Done when", p580-finding.md:88, #793 title · supports: K1, K2 and K3 built now are reused by P583. K5 built now either is the solver's first contact code or a second solver.

- PO-20 · Nobody is blocked without P594 now. P583 #784 (RENT effects) names RENT as its "needed by" and P580's slice 0 as its first step, not P594. D19's order puts P590 and P592 next on the engine. Its value is his interest and the reuse of the kernels later. The board holds 6 of 5 in Ready, and P469 #637 and P511 #706 are in Build (E: effects brief, the item line), so it adds to the WIP.
- PO-21 · P583 and P594 overlap on the kernels. P583's done-when builds particles and heat smoke in C, PIE where it pays, behind one `fx()` call, "in the s3lcd fork". P594's scope builds the same kernels "inside the engine module" (D17), and lists "effects inside a game (P583)" as out of scope · E #784 "Done when", effects brief.md:20 (scope), D17 (engine brief:293) · supports: two cards name two homes for the same kernels. P587 #788 also names "effects ... in their own file" of the s3lcd split (E #788 "Done when"). This needs one owner and one home before code, or the kernels get built twice or moved once.
- PO-22 · P592 overlap. The brief puts "the engine's rigid tip and topple (P592)" out of scope, but "colliding bodies" (K5) is contact code. - PO-23 · P539 #743 overlap: P580 proposes `fx(kind, x, y, **numbers)` as the platform-neutral edge that "the P539 library can carry". The engine brief records three claimants on one C layer: the engine, P539 and P583's effects (★C22) · E p580-finding.md:79, engine brief:82 · supports: the scenes' API shape is the shape P539 and P583 inherit. - PO-24 · P511 #706 overlap: the scenes reach the screen through the s3lcd fork's frame, `blit_buffer` and `alpha_blend_565` (s3lcd.c:186-221), which P511's self-built driver spike may change · E s3lcd/src/s3lcd.c:186-221, #706 body · supports: a scene timed through today's compose path re-measures if P511 or P537 changes the frame or the send.
- PO-25 · D21 makes each feel slice stop until the PO played it. Ten scenes are ten feel slices if each stops alone · E engine brief D21 (the PO, 2026-11-03), memory "feel slices wait for the PO" (11-03 Q12) · supports: the pace of P594 is set by his play sessions, not by the build. - PO-26 · "Special assembler methods" is in his words, and P580 says PIE pays on the compose (about 3.4 times, H), not on the kernels, while a `.S` file in a user C module is still unproven · E #795 (the PO, verbatim), p580-finding.md:8, 97 · H: a scene alone writes opaque pixels (PO-8), where the PIE gain is smallest, so the scenes may show little of what he asked about · supports: a risk of disappointment unless a scene shows C against PIE side by side, as the rain shows C against Python.
- PO-27 · The panel is 2.0 inches and 240 x 320 in RGB565, with 32 levels of red and blue and 64 of green · E README.md:3. - PO-28 · Licence risk from the brief's own invitation: "Feel free to get inspired by other code" (#795). - PO-29 · What must be true (assumptions, H, the PO assistant):
  - A1, the look: the ten effects read as "cool" on this panel at 24 fps (PO-27).
  - A2, the pixel path: C can write effect pixels to the screen cheaply, through `blit_buffer` of a buffer C fills or through the frame by s3lcd.h. Today the engine has neither (PO-4).
  - A3, the transfer: the cost measured alone carries into a game. It does not, by about 6 times for a blend, unless the scene composes over a picture (PO-8).
  - A4, the budget: the HUD's 11 ms leaves room. About 25 ms does (calculated, PO-5).
  - A5, the variety: six kernels give ten looks that he counts as ten (PO-18).
  - A6, the build: a `.S` assembles in the engine module (P580, unproven).
  - A7, the ownership: P583, P587 and P594 agree on one home (PO-21).
  - A8, the solver: K5 does not pre-empt P592 (PO-22).
- PO-30 · The riskiest belief, my opinion: A2 with A3. Effects become cheap only when C writes the pixels, and no such path exists in the engine module yet. Every scene but sparks needs it, and how it is built decides what P583 inherits. The cheapest test: one fire window of 64 x 64 and one of 320 x 40, with the heat kernel in plain C inside the engine module filling a bytearray, drawn by today's `tft.blit_buffer` opaque and then at alpha 128 over a painted background, timed with the rain's HUD on. That is the first scene and P580's slice-0 compose test at once (p580-refuter.md:165-170). The look (A1) is tested in the same run by the PO's eyes · H: built on PO-4, PO-8, P580 slice 0.

- PO-31 · Pre-mortem: "It is a month later, the ten scenes shipped, and the PO is disappointed. Why?" (H, the PO assistant, each tied to a finding)
  1. Ten scenes were three ideas: fire, smoke and burning bodies looked like one effect in three colours, and the ripple and the shockwave like one (PO-18, A5).
  2. Small windows on black looked like a 1990s demo, and none of them is in a game yet. RENT still has its Python bursts, and P583 had not started (PO-20, PO-21).
  3. Assembler was promised and not shown: PIE stayed out, or showed 1.1 times on opaque writes, or the `.S` work ate the month (PO-26).
  4. The costs on screen proved several times too low once P583 composed the effects over RENT's art (PO-8, A3).
  5. Colliding bodies grew into a solver, which collided with P592's contact design and had to be redone (PO-22).
  6. The kernels were built in engine/core, P587 then moved effects into an s3lcd file, and one of them moved again (PO-21).
  7. Ten "ready to play" stops under D21 stretched the work over many days, or scenes got built ahead of his play and were redone after his words, as with P568's swing (PO-25; bench.md P568 shows his "I don't like how it moves unpredictibly").
  8. A licence slip: code taken from an EUPL or all-rights-reserved source landed in the public MIT repository (PO-28).
  9. Feel numbers were constants in C, so each tweak cost a 48 s rebuild and reflash, against D12 (PO-7; bench.md P589 A8 line: 48 s per edit-to-import cycle).
  10. The HUD's 11 ms crowded the effects, and the fps cap hid the gains (PO-5).

- The cost of any kernel on the board: there is no board run for fire, wave, spring water or grains. All are H from P580 or calculated.
- The EUPL-1.2 text: not read. That it is copyleft and incompatible with copying into MIT is model knowledge (H).
- The BSD variant of PixelDust and its full source: only the header's first 40 lines were read.
- The falling-sand projects (hackaday.io 165620, the Arduino blog) and the Tutsplus download (WaterDemo.zip): search summaries and the article page only.
- The board column of P583 #784 and of the related cards, and whether P583 has started: not read (only issue bodies).

## Questions for the decider

- What does "bodies colliding" mean on screen? (a) many grains that pile and flow (K6), (b) a few round bodies that knock each other in a box (K5), (c) RENT's floors (P592) · decides: K5 or K6, and whether the scene touches P592 and D16's solver.
- What does "being on fire" mean? (a) bodies that trail flame as they move (heat injected at their positions, C facts only), (b) a body that catches fire on contact and burns out (a rule, so Python decides it under D12) · decides: scene 3's method and whether it needs events.

- Is assembler (PIE) a goal in itself for you, to be seen in a scene (a BOOT tap from C to PIE, as the rain swaps C and Python), or only where it pays? · decides: whether `.S` work enters P594 and which scene carries it.

- One play session for all ten, in batches of three or four, or a stop after each scene (D21)? · decides: the slice order and the pace.
- Keep the rain's 11 ms text HUD, or a cheaper one (the bar only, or text redrawn twice a second into a kept strip)? · decides: each scene's budget.
- The tenth slot: plasma or clouds, dissolve, glow, or lightning? · decides: the last scene.
