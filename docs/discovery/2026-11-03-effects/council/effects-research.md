# P594 effects research: the gaps P580 left (2026-11-03)

The effects researcher (Opus 5.5) for the P594 discovery (spark #795). Read first: p580-finding.md and p580-refuter.md, the effects brief, engine/core/world.c, bench.md P593. Tags: E evidence with a pointer, H a hypothesis and where it comes from, D a decision (none taken here). All web and `gh api` sources read 2026-11-03. Clock 240 MHz, so 1 ms = 240,000 cycles (E: build-s3lcd/sdkconfig:1592, via p580-finding.md:3). A frame at 24 fps is 41.67 ms (calculated). Per-pixel costs reused from P580 (E: run46 via p580-finding.md:15): keyed write into the PSRAM frame 55 ns/px, C alpha blend 337.5 ns/px, PIE alpha blend about 100 ns/px (H). "Calc" below means calculated by me from a stated cycle count per operation, which is itself H.

## Findings

### 1. Colliding bodies

- FX-1 · Position-based Verlet with a uniform grid. Each body keeps its position and last position. On contact, two equal circles are pushed apart along the line between them by half the overlap each: `delta = 0.5 * (1 - dist)` in units of one diameter, applied only when `dist2 < 1` (no sqrt for a miss). The grid cell is one diameter, and each body tests the 9 cells around its own. The solver runs 8 substeps per frame. · E · github.com/johnBuffer/VerletSFML-Multithread src/physics/physics.hpp:17-46, 55-69, 126-133 (master, read 2026-11-03) · supports only: the method and its constants as one author tuned them for a desktop. It is C++, float, threaded.
- FX-2 · That grid cell holds at most 4 ids, and a 5th body in a cell overwrites the last slot without a warning. · E · VerletSFML-Multithread src/physics/collision_grid.hpp:9-21 · supports only: a caveat if the grid is copied. A count-sorted grid (one head per cell plus one next per body) has no cap (H, model knowledge).
- FX-3 · Cost order of FX-1: O(N) to build the grid plus O(N·k) pair tests per substep, k the bodies in the 9 cells. For 300 circles packed about one per cell, about 9 tests of ~10 cycles plus ~3 contacts of ~40 cycles each: about 210 cycles a body, 63,000 cycles = 0.26 ms a substep, and 2.1 ms a frame at 8 substeps · H calc · cycle counts per test and contact are model knowledge · supports only: an order of magnitude. Grid memory for 8 px cells on 320 x 240: 40 x 30 = 1,200 cells, 2.4 KB of heads plus 600 B of next links at int16 (calc).
- FX-4 · An integer Verlet on an ESP32 exists: positions scaled x100 into `int`, all-pairs collision (two nested loops over balls, a box pre-check, `fastSQRT` on a hit), on a 64 x 32 stage. · E · github.com/jgoergen/HackerBox-0036 arduino/Verlet.ino:2-3, 7-9, 95-130 (pushed 2019-04-11). No licence file (gh api `license` is null) · supports only: that a fixed-point Verlet ran on an ESP32 at that size. No timing was given. Idea only.
- FX-5 · The engine already holds Q16.16 helpers (`to_q`, `q_mul` with an int64 product). · E · engine/core/world.c:8-16 (marked "probe, throwaway (P589)") · supports only: a fixed-point format the team tried in P589. P589 measures float against fixed (engine brief D16).
- FX-6 · Jakobsen's sqrt-free constraint: `delta *= restlength*restlength/(delta*delta+restlength*restlength) - 0.5; x1 -= delta; x2 += delta;`, a first-order Taylor step that is exact when the distance is already right. For cloth "only one iteration is necessary". Verlet update `x' = 2x - x* + a·Δt²`. · E · Thomas Jakobsen, "Advanced Character Physics", GDC 2001, PDF copy at edelkas.github.io/n/files/bib/jakobsen.pdf (19 pages, sha256 ed03e4b89a9c803d6f363bbfdf9b75398343211b7e8a2fde210c13ca3830146b), pdftotext lines 100-120, 364-372, 406-414 · supports only: the formulas. Applying it to circle contact (rest length = 2r, known) is my inference (H). The paper carries no licence. Idea only.
- FX-7 · Box2D-Lite's broad phase is all pairs: two nested loops over `bodies` (O(N²)), then `iterations` passes of sequential impulses over all arbiters and joints. Its source is 826 lines of C++ (World 136, Collide 328, Arbiter 197, Joint 109, Body 56), boxes only. · E · github.com/erincatto/box2d-lite src/World.cpp:44-51, 86-133 (pushed 2020-01-10), `wc -l` of the five src files · supports only: the shape and size of the method.
- FX-8 · Box2D-Lite cost order at a few hundred bodies: 300 bodies give 44,850 pair tests a step. At ~15 cycles each that is 2.8 ms a step and 11 ms a frame at the engine's 10 ms step (4.17 steps a frame). At 30 boxes: 435 pairs, negligible. · H calc · supports only: Box2D-Lite fits tens of boxes, not hundreds, unless its broad phase is replaced by FX-3's grid or FX-9.
- FX-9 · Sort and sweep (sweep and prune): sort interval ends on one axis with insertion sort each step. Average O(n + m) (m the overlapping pairs), O(n) best on a nearly sorted list, O(n²) worst when the order reverses. · E · leanrada.com/notes/sweep-and-prune-2/ (dated 6 Aug 2023), no licence on the page · supports only: the cost order. A collapsing pile or bodies that swap order on the sort axis give the worst case (H, from the stated worst case).
- FX-10 · Licences: Box2D-Lite repo LICENSE is MIT, "Copyright (c) 2019 Erin Catto". Each source file also carries the older notice "Copyright (c) 2006-2009 Erin Catto ... Permission to use, copy, modify, distribute and sell ... provided that the above copyright notice appear in all copies". VerletSFML and VerletSFML-Multithread are MIT (gh api). · E · gh api repos/erincatto/box2d-lite (license MIT), LICENSE:1-3, src/World.cpp:1-10. gh api repos/johnBuffer/VerletSFML-Multithread (MIT, pushed 2026-10-25) · supports only: take with notice, both notices for Box2D-Lite.
- FX-11 · Drawing hundreds of bodies costs more than stepping them when Python draws: the rain draws 160 boxes in 13.47 to 14.01 ms a frame and reads them back in 1.06 to 1.14 ms, against a 0.16 to 0.17 ms C step. · E · bench.md P593 table (firebeetle-games bench.md:266-271) · supports only: for hundreds of colliding bodies the draw has to be in C. A C draw of 300 circles of r = 3 (about 28 px each, 8,400 px) at 55 ns/px keyed is 0.46 ms (H calc).
- FX-12 · PIE adds little to any of FX-1, FX-7, FX-9: the grid lookup and contact pairs are gathers, and PIE has no gather. · H · from p580-finding.md:35 (no gather) · supports only: plain C for the collision step.

### 2. Bodies on fire

- FX-13 · The heat-diffusion fire is fed by setting the source pixels of the buffer each frame. A moving burning body writes heat at its position each frame (my reading). The 1999 article does not cover a moving source. · H · search-result summary of Seumas McNally, "Fire Special FX", archive.gamedev.net/archive/reference/articles/article719.html. The page itself was behind a bot check (403, and curl got a Cloudflare challenge) · supports only: that seeding a heat buffer is the classic way to start fire.
- FX-14 · Particle emitters are attached to moving objects in common engines: an emitter follows an object at an offset, and "rate over distance" lays particles along its path. · H · search-result summary of phaser.io/packs/particles-2d.md, not read · supports only: that emitters on bodies are a common pattern.
- FX-15 · A falling-sand engine burns by cells: a fire cell loses 2 or 3 of its life byte `ra` each tick, moves to a random empty neighbour, ignites gas or dust neighbours at `ra = 150 + (dx+dy)·10`, and dies when `ra < 5` or when it touches water. · E · github.com/MaxBittker/sandspiel crate/src/species.rs:591-630 at 9936e06b, MIT · supports only: a fourth way, fire as cells in a grid, which fits only if the water effect is also a cell grid (FX-24).
- FX-16 · Four ways to attach fire to a moving body, with their cost and which reuses P580's rank-1 heat kernel. (a) One screen-space heat buffer seeded at each burning body: reuses the kernel as is. At half resolution, 160 x 120 = 19,200 B, the kernel at 30 cycles/px is 2.4 ms and a 2x upscaled keyed compose of 76,800 px at 55 ns is 4.2 ms, about 6.6 ms a frame, whatever the number of bodies. Flames trail behind a moving body, because heat rises in screen space. (b) A small heat window per body (for example 16 x 32 = 512 px) that moves with it: reuses the kernel. About 0.21 µs/px (30 + 8 cycles plus 55 ns), 0.11 ms a body, 1.1 ms for 10 bodies. The window's contents move with the body, so it does not trail. (c) Fire particles emitted from the body: reuses P580's rank 2 (particles), not the kernel. 2 per frame with a life of 12 frames is 24 live per body, 240 for 10 bodies, about 0.12 ms drawn as 3 x 3 dots. (d) A sprite flame flipbook: a keyed blit, 23.76 µs warm or 67.80 µs interleaved for 16 x 24 (E: run46 via p580-refuter.md:137). No kernel. · H calc, with the per-pixel inputs E from P580 · supports only: (a) and (b) are the two that reuse the heat kernel. Which looks right is an eye test.

### 3. Water

- FX-17 · The spring-column surface ("Make a Splash"): each column is a spring toward a target height, `acceleration = -k·x` with `k = 0.025` and a damping of 0.025. The columns then pull their neighbours by `Spread` (0 to 0.5), measured into `leftDeltas` and `rightDeltas` before any height changes, repeated 8 times a step. A splash sets one column's speed. Splash particles appear only when speed > 60, `speed / 8` of them. The demo used 201 springs across 800 px. Water is drawn as one trapezoid per column gap, darker at the bottom. Droplets are drawn additively into a target and alpha-tested, "so nearby droplets merge like metaballs". · E · Michael Hoffman, code.tutsplus.com/make-a-splash-with-dynamic-2d-water-effects--gamedev-236t (2012-08-14). No licence for the code. Footer "© 2025 Envato" · supports only: the method and its constants. Idea only.
- FX-18 · Cost order of FX-17: O(C·passes). 80 columns (4 px each across 320) x 8 passes is about 13,600 cycles, 0.06 ms. The cost is the fill: an opaque 320 x 80 body is 25,600 px x 55 ns = 1.4 ms. Redrawing only the surface band (± 10 px) is 6,400 px, 0.35 ms, if the background under it is kept. Translucent water at 337.5 ns/px is 8.6 ms in C, about 2.6 ms with a PIE blend. · H calc, per-px inputs E from P580 · supports only: a filled level fits a 24 fps frame in C if opaque, and needs the PIE blend if translucent.
- FX-19 · A MIT port of the spring-column method exists in Lua. · E · gh api repos/azoyan/water-waves-love2d (MIT, 7 stars, pushed 2018-01-31) · supports only: a licensed port of an unlicensed tutorial. Whether the port is derived code was not checked. Idea only (H on provenance).
- FX-20 · The 2D heightfield (two-buffer ripple, Hugo Elias) is covered by P580 rank 3: a top-down surface, 64 x 64 at about 0.8 ms. · E · p580-finding.md:21, 47 · supports only: it is the "seen from above" water. FX-17 is "seen from the side".
- FX-21 · Falling-sand water: a water cell falls down, else diagonally down to a random side, else to the other side, else "scoots" two cells sideways when both are empty. A direction bit (`ra % 2`) is shared with water neighbours. The grid is scanned left to right and top to bottom, and a clock byte stops a cell moving twice in one tick. Each cell is a 4-byte struct (species, ra, rb, clock). The game ran at 250 x 250, then 300 x 300, which the author calls a mistake: many devices did not hold 60 fps at that size. · E · sandspiel crate/src/species.rs:167-215 at 9936e06b, crate/src/lib.rs:173-187. Maxbittker.com/making-sandspiel (Max Bittker, 2019-04-30) · supports only: the rules and that grid size is the cost lever.
- FX-22 · Licence of FX-21: MIT, "Copyright (c) 2018 Max Bittker". · E · gh api repos/MaxBittker/sandspiel (MIT), LICENSE:1-3 · supports only: take with notice. The source is Rust, so it is a port, not a copy.
- FX-23 · Cost order of FX-21: O(cells) per step. 160 x 120 cells of 2 x 2 px at ~20 cycles a cell is 1.6 ms a step. 320 x 240 at 1 px is 6.4 ms. At 1 B a cell (species and a direction bit packed), 160 x 120 is 19,200 B, 60 % of the 31,744 B largest internal block, or it lives in PSRAM. · H calc · supports only: half resolution fits a 24 fps frame in C. PIE does not help: each cell's move depends on its neighbours' moves in the same scan (H, model knowledge).
- FX-24 · Particle fluid, Clavet's double density relaxation: per neighbour within radius h, `q = r/h`, density `+= (1-q)²`, near density `+= (1-q)³`. Then `P = k(ρ - ρ0)`, `P_near = k_near·ρ_near`. Displacement `D = Δt²·(P(1-q) + P_near(1-q)²)·n`, half to each particle. A spatial hash keeps it O(N·k). One implementation ran about 9,000 particles at 25 to 40 fps on a 4-core i7 with OpenMP. · E · Jan Scheffczyk, blog-46o.pages.dev/posts/2017-08-29-fluidsim (2017-09-04), footer "All rights reserved". The method is Clavet, Beaudoin, Poulin, "Particle-based Viscoelastic Fluid Simulation", SCA 2005 (not read, cited by the blog and search results) · supports only: the formulas. Idea only.
- FX-25 · Cost order of FX-24 on the S3: 200 particles, ~12 neighbours, 2 neighbour passes at ~60 float cycles a pair: 288,000 cycles = 1.2 ms a step, 4.8 ms a frame at 4 steps. Metaball drawing: splat each particle (r = 6, 113 px) additively into an 8-bit field in internal RAM, 22,600 px at ~5 cycles = 0.47 ms, then a threshold pass over a 160 x 100 window, 16,000 px at 55 ns plus a compare, about 1.0 ms. About 6 ms a frame. · H calc · supports only: 200 particles fit. Past about 500 it does not fit at 4 steps (H, linear scaling).
- FX-26 · The metaball threshold pass is a compare and select per pixel, the shape that fits PIE's 16-bit compare and AND/OR select (the keyed compose of P580). · H · from p580-finding.md:35 (VCMP.EQ/GT.S16 and ANDQ/ORQ, E there) · supports only: a place where PIE can pay in water.
- FX-27 · Position-based fluids (Macklin and Müller 2013) were not read. They iterate a density constraint several times per step, so they cost a multiple of FX-24. · H · model knowledge · supports only: skip for this board unless FX-24 looks wrong.

### 4. Candidates to fill ten

- FX-28 · Lightning by a jagged line: random points along the line, each displaced sideways, neighbouring displacements limited ("sway" 80, "jaggedness" 1/80), an envelope that pins both ends. 3 to 6 branches at 30° from random points. Glow from additive line segments or a post-process glow. · E · Michael Hoffman, code.tutsplus.com/so-erzeugen-sie-schockierend-gute-2d-blitzeffekte--gamedev-2681t (2013-01-08), no licence for the code · supports only: the method. Idea only. Cost: O(segments). 128 segments as ~400 px of lines at 55 ns is 22 µs, plus P580's glow on a window (rank 4, about 0.5 ms with PIE, 2.4 ms in C) (H calc).
- FX-29 · Plasma: a sum of sines in x, y and distance, computed once into an 8-bit buffer. Each frame only the palette index shifts: `(plasma[y][x] + paletteShift) % 256`. · E · lodev.org/cgtutor/plasma.html, "Copyright (c) 2004-2007 by Lode Vandevenne. All rights reserved." · supports only: the method. Idea only. Cost: a palette lookup and a write per pixel, 64 x 64 window 4,096 px x (8 cycles + 55 ns) = 0.36 ms. Full screen 6.8 ms (H calc). The lookup is a gather, so scalar.
- FX-30 · Rope and cloth: Verlet points with distance constraints relaxed in a loop (FX-6), one iteration enough for cloth. A rope of 32 points at 4 iterations is about 3,700 cycles (15 µs). A 16 x 12 cloth of ~350 edges at 2 iterations about 0.09 ms, plus ~0.11 ms of lines (H calc). · E for the method (FX-6), H for the cost · supports only: rope and cloth are cheap. Idea only.
- FX-31 · Starfield warp: each star has x, y, z. Z drops by its speed each frame and the star respawns far away below a threshold. Screen position `cx + x·(w/z)`, `cy + y·(w/z)`. A streak from the last to the new position gives the warp look. · H · search-result summaries of dev.to/desmo/a-rust-powered-starfield-in-the-browser-with-macroquad-wasm-4hh3 and benebsworth.pages.dev/lab/starfield/, not opened · supports only: the method. 200 stars at one divide and a 4 px streak each is under 0.1 ms (H calc).
- FX-32 · A cheaper fire kernel than Lode's: each pixel copies the pixel below into the row above at a random sideways offset of -1 to +2, minus `rand & 1`. 37 palette entries, white to black through yellow, orange and red. The bottom row is the never-updated source. · E · Fabien Sanglard, fabiensanglard.net/doom_fire_psx/ (2018-12-28). No licence on the page. Its repo fabiensanglard/DoomFirePSX has no licence (gh api `license` null) · supports only: one read, one random and one write a pixel against Lode's five reads (H: about a third of the cycles). It reuses the same heat buffer and palette path as P580 rank 1. Idea only.
- FX-33 · Explosion with debris is a composition of parts already costed: a particle burst (P580 rank 2), a shockwave ring (P580 rank 3, or a drawn expanding circle), heat seeded at the centre (rank 1 or FX-32), and screen shake (rank 6). · H · composition of p580-finding.md:19-24 · supports only: no new kernel needed.
- FX-34 · Sparks off a collision: particles spawned at each contact the solver reports, with speed and direction from the contact. The engine's event path (C writes kind, body id, speed, x, y as ints into a buffer each step) is the hook. · E for the event path · docs/discovery/2026-11-03-engine/brief.md:100 (Q4 E1, answered D13) · supports only: sparks need no new data. The C side can spawn them without Python if the effect owns them (Q13 yes, effects brief).
- FX-35 · Smoke trails: either fading, growing particles dropped along a path (rank 2), or heat seeded into a grey-palette heat buffer at the moving point (FX-16 a, which trails by itself). · H · from FX-16 and p580-finding.md:46 (smoke = the same kernel with a grey palette) · supports only: no new kernel.
- FX-36 · Rain splashes: the P593 rain plus 2 to 4 short-lived particles at each impact. · H · from bench.md P593 and P580 rank 2 · supports only: a small step on what plays today.

### 5. esp-dsp and new PIE code

- FX-37 · esp-dsp's S3 (aes3) paths are dot products, FIR, FFT, IIR, element-wise add, sub and mul (s8, s16), matrix multiply (s16, f32), memcpy and memset. Image functions are `dspi_dotprod_*` (two image patches into one scalar) and `dspi_conv_f32`. `dspi_conv_f32` is ANSI C and float only. `dsps_mulc` and `dsps_addc` (multiply or add a constant) have no aes3 path, only ae32. · E · gh api git tree of espressif/esp-dsp master (3c8ac0fd, 2026-05-11. Latest tag v1.8.2), modules/conv/include/dspi_conv.h:42-52, modules/dotprod/include/dspi_dotprod.h:66-79 · supports only: what exists.
- FX-38 · Which apply to pixel effects: `dsps_add_s8_aes3` / `_s16_aes3` as a saturating add of two 8-bit or 16-bit planes (glow, combining heat). `dsps_mul_s16_aes3` element-wise multiply with shift (a decay mask over a heat buffer). `dsps_memset_aes3` / `dsps_memcpy_aes3` to clear a buffer or restore a snapshot. `dspi_dotprod_*` reduces a whole patch to one number, So a 3 x 3 convolution needs one call per output pixel, and the call overhead dominates. `dspm_mult_s16` transforms points, not pixels. · H · from the signatures in FX-37 · supports only: esp-dsp offers buffer arithmetic, not a pixel kernel.
- FX-39 · `dsps_add_s8_aes3` takes its S3 path only when all three steps are 1, both inputs are 16-byte aligned and the length is a multiple of 16. The S3 path is `ee.vadds.s8` (signed, saturating at 127) and never applies `shift`. The fallback path loads with `l8ui` (unsigned), adds, shifts right and wraps. So the two paths give different results for the same call. Its header comment names `int16_t` arguments for the s8 function. `dsps_mul_s16_aes3.S` carries the same add-loop comment. · E · esp-dsp modules/math/add/fixed/dsps_add_s8_aes3.S:14-22, 38-98. Modules/math/mul/fixed/dsps_mul_s16_aes3.S:14-22 · supports only: a caveat. Heat in 0 to 255 saturates at 127 on the S3 path.
- FX-40 · Measured S3 cycle counts for image dot products: `dspi_dotprod_s8/u8` on two 64 x 64 images 689 cycles on the S3 against 37,962 ANSI (55 x). 16 x 16: 161 against 2,633. `dspi_dotprod_s16` 32 x 32: 409 against 15,181. `dspm_mult_s16` 16 x 16: 2,004 against 71,112. -O2. · E · esp-dsp docs/en/esp-dsp-benchmarks.rst:74, 87-101 · supports only: PIE gains of this size on a pure streaming reduction. The memory (internal or PSRAM) and cache state are not stated.
- FX-41 · esp-dsp licence: Apache-2.0, with SPDX headers in each `.S` (`SPDX-License-Identifier: Apache-2.0`, Espressif 2023 or 2024). · E · gh api repos/espressif/esp-dsp (Apache-2.0). Dsps_add_s8_aes3.S:2-4. Dsps_memcpy_aes3.S:2-4 · supports only: take with notice (keep the header, add a NOTICE line, as P580's refuter put it for esp_lvgl_port).
- FX-42 · New PIE pixel code found that P580's search missed: BitsForPeople/esp-bits-and-pieces `esp32-s3/rgb565/rgb565_s3_simd.hpp` (30,528 B) and `s3_simd_qtil.hpp`, created 2026-10-15, last commits 2026-10-16 and 17 ("Added more SIMD operations", "corrected asm in from565Masked()"). It holds RGB565 unpack into planar 8-bit R, G, B vectors, blend (`EE.VMULAS.U8.QACC` then `EE.SRCMB.S8.QACC` with shift 8), scale, add, add with clamp, rotate. Licence GPL-3.0. · E · gh api repos/BitsForPeople/esp-bits-and-pieces (GPL-3.0, 0 stars), commits on esp32-s3/rgb565, rgb565_s3_simd.hpp:184-232, LICENSE:1-2 · supports only: a planar-RGB blend design on PIE exists. Idea only (GPL).
- FX-43 · That code uses GCC inline `asm` in C++ with the Q registers hard-coded (q0 to q7 never appear as operands), and orders the asm statements by a dummy memory operand (`"=m" (dep)`). · E · rgb565_s3_simd.hpp:192-232 · supports only: P580's "cannot be inline-asm operands" holds, but inline asm with fixed Q registers is used in the wild. - FX-44 · A claim of "blending 16+16 pixels in roughly 90 to 100 cycles, about 5 x faster than scalar" for that code. · H · search-result summary of github.com/CubeCoders/Jet/discussions/10, not opened · supports only: a number to check, not a measurement.

## What I could not check, and why

- McNally's "Fire Special FX" (1999) and the gamedev.net lightning blog: both 403 to WebFetch, and the archive answers curl with a Cloudflare challenge. FX-13 rests on a search summary.
- Clavet et al. 2005 itself: not opened. The formulas come from Scheffczyk's blog, which follows the paper's Algorithm 2.
- The starfield pages, the Phaser emitter page and the CubeCoders/Jet discussion: search summaries only (FX-14, FX-31, FX-44).
- Whether esp-dsp builds as an ESP-IDF component inside a MicroPython user C module, and what its `.S` files cost from PSRAM buffers: needs a build and the board.
- Every cost marked H calc: cycle counts per operation are model knowledge. None was measured on the board. Only the per-pixel write and blend costs (from run46) and esp-dsp's own cycle counts are measured.
- Whether azoyan/water-waves-love2d is derived from the unlicensed tutorial (FX-19).
- georgik/raylib on the Espressif component registry appeared in a result. It was not read, so whether it holds S3 pixel paths is —.
- Box2D-Lite's default iteration count and its contact count per box pair were not read beyond World.cpp.
- No search for a PIE-friendly cellular-automaton or Verlet layout. FX-12 and FX-23 rest on the absence of a gather (P580).

## Questions for the decider

- Is "bodies colliding" hundreds of circles (Verlet grid) or tens of boxes that stack and tip (Box2D-Lite style)? · decides: the solver for the scene, and whether it is the engine's world.c (D16) or a separate effect.
- Does a burning body trail flames behind it (one screen buffer, FX-16 a) or carry its flames with it (a window per body, FX-16 b)? · decides: which heat buffer layout the kernel gets, and the cost (fixed about 6.6 ms against about 0.11 ms a body).
- Which water: a filled level seen from the side with waves and splashes (FX-17), a surface seen from above (P580 ripple), pouring water as cells (FX-21) or as particles with a soft edge (FX-24)? · decides: one or two of four methods, and whether water shares a grid with fire (FX-15).
- Is an Apache-2.0 component allowed in an effect scene (esp-dsp, with its notice), or only own code learned from formulas? · decides: whether esp-dsp's buffer arithmetic is linked or the same few vector loops are written in our own `.S`.
- Is translucent water or glow allowed in a scene (it needs the PIE blend to fit), or only opaque and keyed drawing in C? · decides: whether the PIE compose is in the first slices or later.
- Does the effects work need ten new scenes, or count the rain and compositions (explosion, sparks, rain splashes) toward ten? · decides: how many new kernels the slices hold.
