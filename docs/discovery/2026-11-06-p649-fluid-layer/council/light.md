### RA: risks and assumptions

- RA-1 · Riskiest: "one flow carries any material". Heat/smoke/steam as passive scalars share advection (EX-2). Liquid water (P597 spring surface) and sand/water cells (P600 CA) do not: they are a height field and a per-cell material id (EX-13, UJ-2) · E for what the cards ask, H that no velocity field helps them · cheapest test: write on one page which of P597, P600, P647, P650 would read `fx_flow`; expect 2 of 4 (fire, smoke) · supports narrowing the MVP claim to "any advected quantity".
- RA-2 · "A per-cell velocity field fits the frame budget": at full resolution a flow lookup is about the cost of a second field (+5 to 7 ms, FE-3), and the fire has under 0.5 ms spare on the draw pass line (P646: misses D5 by 0.28 ms) · E for P646's miss, H for FE-3 · cheapest test: Mac op count, then one scratch board probe like run120 with a 40 x 30 velocity grid sampled by the heat step; read ms, not fps.
- RA-3 · "Heat and water need the same advection": heat rises (buoyancy, sinks cooling, bound `hot`), smoke drifts and fades, water flows down and conserves mass. A shared advect + per-quantity source/sink is the Stam model (density and temperature in Stam-style demos), but the fire's `hot` bound and random draws are look-critical · H (Stam 2003 shape; EX-2) · cheapest test: Mac: run the fire through `fx_advect` with the legacy offset and compare bytes; then run a second quantity with other sinks and look at clips.
- RA-4 · "One renderer fits fire's palette and water's look": the renderer is a lookup (palette[value], alpha[value]) with GL_LINEAR; fire's value is heat, water would need depth/density mapped to colour. The picture-mode blend costs 330 ns per translucent pixel in the frame (bench.md:217, driver blit at alpha 128), so a see-through water layer over the full screen is a 2 to 3 ms+ pass each (H sum) · E for the 330 ns/px, H for the sum · cheapest test: Mac frames of a water palette from the same renderer; then draw ms on the board.
- RA-5 · Determinism: the fire's tests repeat the rng draw order byte for byte. A flow that consumes draws (wander uses one per step) or a solver in float changes bytes; a float solver on the Mac vs the S3 can differ in the last bit · E (fx_fire.c draw-order comment; test_fire_core.py exists) · cheapest test: pick fixed-point for the flow from the start (FE-4's forum note: fixed point about 3x faster than float, claim only) and test the Mac C against a Python reference.
