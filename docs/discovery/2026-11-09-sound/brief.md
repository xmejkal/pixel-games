- [★C2] MicroPython v1.29.0's `machine.I2S` is a thin shell over ESP-IDF `i2s_std`: Philips mode only, MCLK unused, no PDM and no TDM exposed. It moves bytes. It does not mix, loop, fade or resample, and `I2S.shift` scales a whole buffer in C (~/esp/micropython-v1.29.0/extmod/machine_i2s.c:442) · E ~/esp/micropython-v1.29.0/ports/esp32/machine_i2s.c:361-399 (FEAS-1, FEAS-2, FEAS-9) · caveat: the source was read, and it was not run on this board
- [★C3] Buffer depth moves in 16 ms blocks at 16 kHz. Mono TX keeps a 2-byte DMA frame, so `ibuf` 2048 B is 4 blocks and 4096 B is 8 blocks, which is 64 ms and 128 ms of capacity (calculated). The driver's queue is one block short of that, and a partly filled last block plays as zeros, so 4096 B covers a stall of about 104 to 128 ms (calculated, not run) · E ~/esp/micropython-v1.29.0/ports/esp32/machine_i2s.c:134-155, ~/esp/esp-idf-v5.5.2/components/esp_driver_i2s/i2s_common.c:329 · caveat: re-read here. FEAS-3 reads mono as expanded to stereo. The TX DMA frame is not expanded, and the wire still carries two slots (machine_i2s.c:372). Not run on the board
- [★C4] The buffer that hides a stall delays every new sound. Events are polled once per frame, so a hit waits up to one frame plus the buffer: about 104 ms with 64 ms of buffer and about 168 ms with 128 ms in a 40 ms frame, and about 233 ms in Road's 105 ms frame (calculated). Of the two depths named, only 128 ms nominally covers Road's 105 ms gap, and the driver's queue makes the coverage about 104 to 128 ms (★C3) · H calculated from ★C1 (one run), ★C3, FEAS-11, RA-5 · caveat: the 20 to 30 ms at which a delay is noticed is model knowledge (RA-5), and broadcast limits for late audio are wider (R3, H, not opened). 40 ms is the frame target (games/app.py:31), and Road averages 43 to 50 ms. An own ring that re-mixes close to the read head hides a stall with less delay (R2, H). Not measured on this board
- [★C5] The reports split on whether Python can fill samples one by one. The PO assistant says a naive version "cannot loop per sample" (EX-7, and RA-4 repeats it, from 57 µs per particle and about 80 µs per canvas call, measured on other code). The tech lead calculates one voice at 16 kHz as 1.28 ms (2 µs per sample, a guess) to 6.4 ms (10 µs, an estimate) per 40 ms frame (FEAS-7). Fire leaves 17.6 ms of the frame, so one voice fits up to 27.5 µs per sample in Fire, as a mean of one 5 s window. Other games leave less (9.3 to 9.6 ms in Burning, Flappy and Runner, 2.5 to 7.1 ms in Cave), and Road leaves none (−0.7 and −6.5 ms). The whole CPU is spent at 62.5 µs (calculated). The 10 µs estimate is close to one float statement by its own derivation (56.8 µs over 7 statements in `probe567.py`, about 8 µs each, calculated), and a sample takes several. An integer statement on an `array('h')` has no measured cost, so whether 6.4 ms is low stays open (Q6) · H EX-7, RA-4, FEAS-7, run130-p656-smoke.log:12 · caveat: no per-sample cost is measured on this board. The 17.6 ms comes from the means of one run. FEAS-7 used Stack's 10.8 ms headroom (run52) and says "two voices do not fit", and this brief uses Fire's 17.6 ms (run130). At 32 kHz the cost is 12.8 ms at 10 µs, and Fire's budget falls to 13.75 µs per sample (calculated). The loop body sets the cost, not one global figure (R4)
