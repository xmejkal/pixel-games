- **TL-1** The restyle has no frame-time headroom to spend. The pace is 40 ms, paint plus step is already 39 to 43 ms, and fps is 20 to 24. Every added ms of sprites, layers or particles is paid by a cut or by lower fps. The brief's outcome line ("fps does not fall below P556") therefore makes the order of the driver cards a precondition for the wild art, not a nicety. · E · origin/p556-double-buffer bench.md pace bullets; brief.md outcome line · supports: the dependency order below.
- **TL-2** A full-frame parallax scroll caps the frame at about 32 fps whatever the paint costs. Every layer moves, so every frame is a full send; the wire is 30.72 ms at the real 40 MHz, and 80 MHz gave a dark panel. 1000 / (3.3 + 31.4) = 28.8 fps by the frame model's copy + send once paint falls under the send. A kept background (P554 #758) does not help a scrolling game: its background changes every pixel each frame. It helps a static-backdrop game (stack, snake, pong). · E for the clock (bench.md:33, :38, :84); calculated for the rest · supports: which games can be "wild" and still reach the P556 fps, and that parallax games stop at about 29 fps.
- **TL-3** All blit costs in bench.md were measured with the framebuffer internal. P556 now paints into PSRAM, which costs +8.4 to +9.7 ms on the same art. The PSRAM-destination cost of an opaque layer, a keyed layer and a sprite is not measured. The 7.8 ms scene sum above is an internal-destination lower bound. · E (P562 runs 34/35 predate "double"; P556 slice 0) · supports: the need for one probe (below) before sizing a scene; it does not support any per-layer number in PSRAM.
- **TL-4** The unattributed part of today's paint is large. Flappy paints in 32.5 ms (dma, run39, mostly game-over screen) while the layers sum to about 7.8 ms and the game-over box is 33 to 49 ms. In play with the P562 art, the split of paint between layers, pillars, bird and hud is not in bench.md ("Fps in play waits on the PO's play"). Sizing "how many layers" needs that split first. · E · bench.md:177; p537-finding.md · supports: slice 0 style measurement (average per phase with art on) as the first step; says nothing about play.
- **TL-5** Sprites are cheap in C and expensive in Python. 1 ms holds about 19,400 keyed px; a 16x24 sprite is 20 us of C. The Python wrapper (about 40 to 80 us, H) dominates. At about 60 to 100 us per sprite, 4 ms of sprite budget holds 40 to 65 sprites of 16x24; the same 4 ms holds about 200 sprites of C-only cost. The count rises 3 to 4 x only if P537 slice 3 (sprites in C, batch) lands. Sprite frames cost memory, not time: 16x24 = 768 B, 32x48 = 3,072 B; a hero of 8 states x 6 frames at 32x48 = 147,456 B (calculated). · E for C cost and call cost; H for the wrapper figure · supports: that animation frame count is bounded by art effort and pool size, not by frame time.
- **TL-6** Translucency is the expensive primitive. Alpha below 255, and any blit partly off screen, takes the per-pixel blend path (126 ns/px; a 32x32 smoke puff = 0.13 ms, a full-screen tint = 9.65 ms). The art pipeline writes hard masks only, asserted at paint.py:1403. Glow, smoke, soft shadows and fades must be faked with dithered keyed sprites or pre-tinted frames, or wait for P537 slices 6 and 7 (soft edges, blend modes). A screen flash is a `fill` (2.9 ms). · E · s3lcd/src/s3lcd.c:633-648; art/paint.py (main, the `assert p[3] in ((0, 255)...)` line); bench.md:167 · supports: which "shocking" effects are in reach with no driver work.
- **TL-7** Partly-off-screen sprites take the slow path. Keyed art that enters or leaves the screen edge (every scrolling obstacle, every shake offset) drops from the memcpy/key loop to the alpha path. The cost per pixel on that path with alpha 255 is not measured (it calls `alpha_blend_565` per pixel with bounds checks). · E s3lcd.c:633-648 for the branch; — for its cost · supports: a probe; the P564 note "pillars placed 240 long, the driver clips them" (bench.md:176) means pillars are always on this path.
- **TL-8** PSRAM and pool at ten games. Three games take 1,506,384 B, 502,128 B per game (calculated). Ten games at that average: 5.02 MB against 6.81 MB IDF free, leaving about 1.79 MB, less the "double" frame (153,600 B, in PSRAM) — it is unknown whether 6.81 MB already excludes it — and whatever MicroPython's heap holds. The MicroPython heap and the pool are both PSRAM: how much of the 6.81 MB the heap already took is not in any log (stock heap free 8,319,680 B, bench.md:26). `load()` reads every game's art into the pool at boot in one call, so boot time grows with the art (1.5 MB read time not logged). · E for inputs; calculated for the sum; — for the heap split and the boot time · supports: a probe, and the "pool sized once" note in TL-9.
- **TL-9** The pool grows only by hard reset, and is sized by the first call. `s3lcd.pool(size)` allocates on the first call and raises "the pool is smaller: reset the board" for any later larger size. Adding a game's art (every restyle step) means copy files, then a hard reset, as P562/P564 did. A fixed oversize request at the first call (a constant in pictures.py or a reserve in C) removes the reset; this is a code change, not measured. · E · s3lcd/src/s3lcd.c:2927-2949; bench.md:176 · supports: a small refactor card, not a blocker.
- **TL-10** Internal RAM left after "double" is 31,744 B in the largest DMA block (about 107 KB DMA-capable free in total, calculated 264,347 - 156,672). A kept background (153,600 B) cannot be internal; P554 must keep it in PSRAM and copy dirty tiles into the internal buffer. Any C particle array, glyph cache or tile table must be under 31 KB per block or sit in PSRAM. Non-DMA internal RAM free is not logged. · E probe0-memory.log:1; bench.md P556 slice 1a · supports: a constraint on P554/P557 designs and on "particles in C".
- **TL-11** Flash: art at ten games is about 5 MB (calculated above) in a 16 MB part with a 1.87 MB image. The file-system partition size is not logged; `os.statvfs("/")` settles it in one line. The 1.5 MB of `.bin` already committed in `games/art` grows by the same factor in git on every regeneration that changes a byte. · E for the sizes; — for the partition · supports: a cheap read before art is planned; "never commit a vendor file" (AGENTS.md of spark) is untouched, the art is ours.
- **TL-12** How art is made scales by hand-written Python per game, not by data. paint.py is 1,452 lines for three games (stack about 165 lines, 735-900; flappy about 290 lines, 900-1190; runner and the shared helpers the rest, counted from `grep -n "^def "`). Extrapolating 165 to 290 lines per game for seven more gives 1,155 to 2,030 lines (calculated, H for the extrapolation). `main()` hard-codes the three games' lists and writes `LAYER_SETS` with a literal `"flappy"` block; a fourth game means editing `main()`, not adding a row. Output is deterministic (seed 562) and runs on the Mac only (PIL). · E · art/paint.py:1378-1452 · supports: that procedural painting scales in effort linearly, and that a generated index (a data table per game) is a refactor the ten-game scope needs first.

Backgrounds (320x240 to 640x100) reduce well. ai-cli is not on this Mac's PATH (`which ai-cli`: not found; the skill exists), its cost is unknown, and D1 forbids a paid tool without his yes. · H (model knowledge on generator consistency, no source read); E for the PATH and D1 · supports: that a mixed route is feasible (generated backgrounds, procedural or hand-made sprites); it does not show generated sprites would fail.
- **TL-15** Fit with the technical direction. P537/P539 direction: panel-independent C layer, "draws, decides nothing", games, speeds and rules stay in Python. Squash and stretch, rotation and sub-pixel motion need scaled or fixed-point blits, which are P537 slice 6 (soft edges) and later, optional. · E · p537-finding.md "What the layer holds" and "What stays in Python"; docs/discovery/2026-11-02-graphics-library/items.md:8-23 · supports: that "physics in C" is a direction change, "draw in C" is not.

### Physics at this speed

- **PHYS-1** The games already integrate by dt. `Game.step` computes `dt = min(ticks_diff, 60) / 1000`, so dt varies from about 25 to 60 ms; worst gaps are 63 to 69 ms (clamped at 60). It is semi-implicit Euler (`vy += a*dt; y += vy*dt`), not a fixed step. · E · games/gfx.py `Game.step`; games/arcade2.py Cave.play · supports: the starting point.
- **PHYS-2** Tunnelling is a real constraint. Per-frame displacement at 300 px/s and dt 0.069 s is 300 x 0.069 = 20.7 px; the thinnest solid in the games' art is a 1 to 2 px edge and sprites are 12 to 24 px (the P537 finding records 4 to 25 px per frame). A bouncing or falling body above about 16 px per step skips over a 16 px thing. Sub-stepping to a fixed 1/60 s step needs 2 to 4 physics steps per frame at dt 0.04 to 0.069 (calculated: 0.069 x 60 = 4.1), multiplying the physics cost by that count. · calculated from E inputs (bench.md run44 gap; p537-finding.md) · supports: a fixed step with a cap on steps per frame costs 2 to 4 x the per-body work; swept tests on the few fast bodies are the cheaper alternative (H, standard method).
- **PHYS-3** Python cost per body is only bounded, not measured. Yardsticks: empty loop 2.10 us per iteration, raw C `fill_rect` 5.61 us, a canvas fill that is about 17 Python statements 40.3 us (bench.md:156-161), so about 2 us per simple statement (calculated). A body update of about 12 float statements is then 25 to 50 us (H: floats may be boxed on this port, which allocates; gc runs every 5 frames, app.py:29). Per frame: 30 bodies = 0.75 to 1.5 ms. A spring or verlet constraint of about 15 statements = 30 to 60 us: a 10-segment rope with 4 relaxation passes = 40 constraints = 1.2 to 2.4 ms. A full 40-particle system at the same rate = 1.0 to 2.0 ms for motion, plus the draw (TL-5). These fit if the budget is a few ms; they do not fit if the pace stays at 40 ms with no cut. · calculated from E yardsticks; the statement count and the float-boxing are H · supports: a board probe with a timed loop settles it; it will not show cost under real GC pressure or on the full game.
- **PHYS-4** `@native` already runs particle motion. `gfx.advance` is `@native` (games/gfx.py ~224) and the other candidate is `@viper` integer code. Viper is integer only, which fits a fixed-point (e.g. 8.8) body update and avoids float boxing. The Mac test runs the same code because `fast.py` makes `native` a no-op; a viper decorator would need the same shim. Speed ratio of viper to bytecode on this chip is not measured here. · E for the native use (games/fast.py, gfx.py) · supports: that a viper integrator is a plausible cheap step before C, and that its gain needs a probe.
- **PHYS-5** What fits Python with no C: dt integration, springs, damped follow, easing, impulse jumps with coyote time, squash and stretch by choosing among pre-drawn frames (the bird already has 3 tilts x 2 wings: games/art_index.py flappy_bird00..21), screen shake as a coordinate offset (free on parallax games, which repaint everything, but it forces a repaint on a kept-background game, TL-2), a screen flash as one fill, up to about 40 particles drawn as 2x2 to 3x3 fills. · E for the art frames and Particles.MAX; H for the rest · supports: a physics list that costs no driver card.
- **PHYS-6** What needs C or a driver slice: scaled blit (squash and stretch from one frame), rotation, sub-pixel placement (8.8 fixed point, P537 slice 6), translucent smoke and glow in volume (TL-6), many particles (above about 60 to 100 at 80 us each, 5 to 8 ms), per-pixel effects such as wobble or palette cycling. Verlet cloth with more than about 50 constraints. · E that blit_buffer has no scale or rotate argument (s3lcd.c:583-590); calculated for the particle count · supports: which "wild" ideas wait on P537 slices 3, 6 and 7.
- **PHYS-7** Frame time jitter limits how physical movement looks more than the integrator does. Steps are 4 to 25 px per frame at 20 to 24 fps and the panel has no tearing-effect line (DFR0664), so the tear stays and a gap of 63 to 69 ms against a 40 ms pace shows as a stutter even with an exact integrator. A fixed-step simulation with the draw interpolated (render at the last two states) hides jitter, but needs a second state copy per body and one frame of latency (about 40 ms, calculated at the pace); a game that the PO plays by reflex (flappy, runner) pays that latency. · E for the numbers (bench.md "P533 slice 2", p537-finding.md TL;DR); H for interpolation as the method · supports: that "great physics like movement" is limited first by the frame-time floor in TL-1/TL-2; the PO's eyes judge it, not a probe.
- **PHYS-8** Input latency of the current loop is not logged. The button is read once per frame at the pace (40 ms) and the send now overlaps, so a press can wait up to one frame (40 to 69 ms) before it moves anything. Jump feel ("coyote time", jump buffering) can be written at the step level and costs no ms. · E that input is read in `App.frame` once per frame (app.py:75-82) · supports: that jump-feel tricks are logic only; whether the board feels responsive is a play test.

## What you could not check, and why

- Frame time in play with the P562/P564 art: bench.md has no play numbers for it ("waits on the PO's play"). I cannot play the board; the cheapest rung is the average-per-phase line (`avg ... static paint fx hud`) while the PO plays flappy and runner, which shows the split per phase but not feel.
- The P556 branch's tree: I read its bench.md with `git show`; I did not check out the branch, so I did not read its code (lcd.py `FRAMEBUFFER = "double"`, the C copy).
- The blit costs into a PSRAM destination, a full keyed blit, the cost of a partly-off-screen blit at alpha 255, and the Python cost of a picture draw: none measured. One probe on the board settles all four (a loop of `blit_buffer` calls under `framebuffer="double"`, as run34/run35 did): it shows per-pixel and per-call cost, not the game's cache behaviour or GC under play.
- Whether PSRAM free (6.81 MB) already excludes the pool and the MicroPython heap: `esp32.idf_heap_info` after `pool(5 MB)` plus `gc.mem_free()` settles it; I did not run the board (read-only brief, no board access).
- Boot time to load a larger pool (flash read rate): not logged.
- File-system partition size (`os.statvfs("/")`): not logged.
- Float boxing and per-statement cost on this MicroPython build: only yardsticks from P550; a timed loop of a body update on the board would settle it.
- The seven unpainted games' draw behaviour (snake, cave, tunnel, road, starfire, cubetiming, pong): I read the first 100 lines of arcade2.py and grep hits only. Tunnel, road, starfire and cubetiming (world3d.py, space3d.py, track.py, 616 lines) draw lines and perspective; I did not read how their art could be painted or whether their phase costs allow sprites. - Generated-image cost and quality: ai-cli is not on PATH and no sample was made; D1 forbids a paid run. Nothing here tests generated sprite consistency.
- Art licence for a public MIT showcase repo if images are generated (viability, outside my lens): not checked.
- Web: no search run, to stay inside what the code answers.

## Questions for the decider

- Q-a: Is "no fps below the P556 numbers" a gate for every restyle step, or is a lower fps for a wilder look acceptable in some games? · decides: whether parallax-heavy scenes (capped near 29 fps, TL-2) and sprite-heavy scenes are allowed before P537 slices 2 and 3 land, or wait for them.
- Q-b: Which games must keep a scrolling parallax, and which can use a static or slow-changing backdrop? · decides: whether P554 #758 (kept background) or P557 (tile layers) carries the restyle, and the per-game frame budget.
- Q-c: Is a rule change acceptable that lets C run motion for particles (a generic emitter), or does "the layer draws, decides nothing" stay? · decides: whether many particles (above about 60) are in scope at all (PHYS-6, TL-15).
- Q-d: May the games pay one frame of latency (about 40 ms) for interpolated, fixed-step physics? · decides: PHYS-2/PHYS-7, fixed step with interpolation or plain dt integration with swept checks.
- Q-e: May the importer for generated images (new code, TL-13) be built, and may ai-cli be tried at a stated cost? · decides: art route: procedural only, generated backgrounds plus procedural sprites, or hand-made pixel art; needs his yes under D1 before any paid run.
- Q-f: Is a one-line oversize reserve in `pictures.py` (pool sized once, no hard reset on art growth) a small card to file now? · decides: whether adding a game's art keeps requiring a hard reset (TL-9).
- Q-g: Does the PO want a probe card before the direction: one board session for the four unmeasured costs (TL-3, TL-4, TL-7, TL-8) and the physics statement cost (PHYS-3)? · decides: whether the per-game budget is sized from measurement or left as the calculated bounds above.
