One shared world vs a theme per game

- PO-27 · Shared world: one palette, one set of ground and sky pieces, one character cast. For: art is made once and reused, so the pool and paint time are smaller (the layer machinery in pictures.py already supports shared strips, games/pictures.py:60-70, though the sets are per game today); the collection reads as one author's work (the showcase aim); a theme can be built up over ten games like a story. Against: ten games in one world are all the same mood, which weakens "shocking" and variety; some games (Tunnel, Road, Starfire, CubeTiming are 3D-like wireframe) do not sit in a side-scroller world; one bad world choice spoils all ten. · H (reasoning) · games/space3d.py, world3d.py headers for the 3D point (E) · supports only: the trade-off list.
- PO-28 · Theme per game: For: each game can have its own topic, palette and mood, which gives variety and lets weak ones be dropped alone; a risky topic is limited to one game; each is a smaller job for one session. Against: ten art sets means ten times the files (pool size, copy time, bench.md:176 shows one copy already failed), and the look may drift; a hiring reader sees no through-line. A middle form is a shared look (one painting method and a palette family) with a theme per game. · H (reasoning) · bench.md:176 for the copy failure (E) · supports only: the trade-off list. A choice is the decider's.

Which social topics fit a one-button game, and which read as cheap or offensive (my H view; the decision is the PO's)

- PO-29 · Aim at systems and situations, not at people or groups. The verb of a one-button game is timing, balance, rising, dropping, dodging, holding. A topic fits when it has such a verb and a pressure that can be seen without text. Fits (H): housing stacked ever higher on weak ground (Stack: tap to drop, wobbling tower, a rent or flood line below); rising water or heat as the world shrinks (Flappy or Cave: hold to rise, a closing gap, a climate or scarcity pressure); surveillance as light or attention that sweeps and finds you (Starfire's sweeping crosshair inverted, Tunnel); queues and paperwork as a conveyor you must time (CubeTiming); automation or attention economy as a feed that speeds up (Runner, Road); information noise (Snake grows as it eats data); inequality as a gap in the ground that you jump (Runner), with no named group. Papers Please-like bureaucracy fits as an invented place with invented rules (PO-11). · H · none · supports only: a candidate list for the decider. None was tested for readability.
- PO-30 · Reads as cheap or offensive (H): any real, living person, party or named group; a real ethnic, religious or national group as the enemy or the obstacle; real wars and atrocities as a score (killing or dodging victims for points; the genocide or camp theme with a jump button); disasters with real victims as a game-over joke; a controversial position with no argument just to shock, which is the "edgy" pattern; self-harm and suicide as fail states; a "wrong" ending with a laugh. Reflex games reward the player for being fast, so mapping a real victim to the obstacle the player is rewarded for dodging or hitting turns the theme into a joke. The risk is higher in a job-application showcase. · H (my reasoning; Papers Please's designer is reported to have generalised away from one regime, PO-11) · none · supports only: a sorting rule for the decider.
